## 1. Topic and one hard idea

**A chatbot doesn't pick the likeliest next word. It rolls weighted dice over the odds, on purpose, and the dial on those dice is called temperature.**

**The film is a puzzle the viewer can try** (Strawberry had the most views as a puzzle; fyp and memory showed that making the viewer the subject works). Line 1 is a contradiction most ChatGPT users have seen: ask the same thing twice and get two answers. Line 2 asks why, and says the search phrase. Line 3 gives the twist: dice, on purpose. Then one object carries the film: **a pair of weighted dice** whose faces are next-word odds (pepperoni big, mushrooms small), plus **a dial** that reshapes them. Turn it down and the favorite almost always wins; turn it up and long shots get a chance. Line 9 is the turn: why not always pick the favorite? Researchers tried, and the text got stuck in a loop (the real 2019 GPT-2 output that repeats "Universidad Nacional Autónoma de México" over and over). The coda is the DM-able twist: even with the dial at zero, one lab asked one model the same thing 1,000 times and got 80 different answers, because busy servers round the math differently. The end, "So ask yours twice. Same answer? Yes or no.", loops straight into "Same question. Two different answers."

**What it builds on, and doesn't repeat.** Next word explained tokens, "guess what comes next", "the cat sat on the mat", training and "where the odds come from". Hallucinate used "a chatbot just guesses the next likely word" and the always-has-a-next-word loop. This film takes those odds as given (one callback: "Every next word has odds") and answers the question neither film asked: **who picks from the odds, and how.** No tokens, no training, no hallucination talk.

**Main search phrase: `why does ChatGPT give different answers`.** Spoken verbatim in line 2 (about 2.4–4.8 s). Show it on screen in the first 2 s and in the caption's first line. Secondary phrases for the SEO agent: `ChatGPT temperature`, `what is temperature in AI`, `AI temperature setting`, `same question different answer ChatGPT`, `why does AI give different answers`, `LLM temperature explained`. I didn't check search volumes.

**DM-able moment:** line `zero`/`eighty`: "But at zero, one lab asked the same thing 1,000 times. 80 different answers." Countable, surprising and checkable (fact 6), and the split is a great picture: at word 103, 992 runs say Feynman was born in "Queens, New York", 8 say "New York City". Both are true; it's the same fact in different words.

**Wording choices tied to sources**
- `ask` names ChatGPT because that's the search phrase and what viewers have seen. The **mechanism** is documented for OpenAI's API (facts 1–2), not for the ChatGPT app. The narration never states the app's temperature setting; no OpenAI page I found gives it, and the brief forbids guessing it.
- `dice` "On purpose": supported by fact 4 (quality depends on randomness) and fact 1/8 (both big APIs default to temperature 1, not 0).
- `odds` / `weighted`: **the pizza odds are made up for the demo.** Screen must carry **EXAMPLE ODDS** on the dice/odds card (≥ 36 px). Numbers: PEPPERONI 60% · MUSHROOMS 25% · PINEAPPLE 15% (sums to 100). Keep "every next word has odds" as the only callback to Next word.
- `down` says "**almost** always wins", not "always": at temperature 0 the favorite always wins in theory (fact 5), and at low non-zero temperatures it just wins more often (facts 1, 3). "Almost" is true for the whole low end and sets up `zero`.
- `up` says long shots "get a chance": higher T, softer distribution (fact 3). Not "it gets creative" (an opinion, not a source).
- `stuck`: "Researchers tried" refers to Holtzman et al. maximizing likelihood (beam search) on GPT-2. That's not literally temperature 0, so the screen label says **ALWAYS THE LIKELIEST WORDS · GPT-2 · HOLTZMAN ET AL., ICLR 2020**, and the re-created excerpt carries **RE-CREATED** and an "…" where it's cut. The paper also says low temperature itself "severely" increases repetition (fact 4), so the line is honest either way.
- `zero` says "one lab asked the same thing": the prompt was "Tell me about Richard Feynman" on the **open model Qwen3**, not ChatGPT. The screen must say **OPEN MODEL QWEN3 · TEMPERATURE 0 · THINKING MACHINES LAB, SEP 2025** in big type in the same frame (Truth lesson: the condition in big type).
- `eighty`: 80 *unique* completions out of 1,000 1,000-token runs; most of them start identical and split late (token 103). Show the split, not 80 wildly different essays. Roll the counter in round steps to **80** (exact number given by the source).
- `eighty` "Busy servers round the math differently" is the lay version of fact 7. Not "random hardware errors", not "cosmic rays".

**Kept out on purpose**
- **The ChatGPT or Claude app's temperature.** No primary source states it; consumer apps may also use other settings, system prompts and memory that change answers.
- **Top-p / nucleus sampling and top-k** in narration. True and sourced (fact 4; OpenAI `top_p` docstring: "0.1 means only the tokens comprising the top 10% probability mass are considered"), but a second dial = a second idea. Pinned comment or a 1-frame chip at most.
- **"Temperature comes from physics"** (Boltzmann distribution). Holtzman cites Ackley et al. 1985 for it; nice but a tangent.
- **Other reasons answers differ** (chat history, memory, model updates, A/B tests). Real, but not the topic; memory had its own film.
- **The Claude 4.7 "can't turn it down" fact** in narration: true, primary and 5 days old (fact 8), but it's developer-API detail and would name a second product. Pinned-comment candidate.
- **"Temperature 0 is deterministic."** False in practice (facts 5–7); the film says the opposite.

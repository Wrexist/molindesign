## 1. Topic and one hard idea

**Wi-Fi is radio, waving about 2.4 billion times a second, and most building materials let it through. Higher frequencies lose more in each wall, metal blocks it worst, and water soaks it up, which is exactly what your microwave oven uses to cook, on the same band. So where your router sits decides how much reaches you.**

Story order: the contradiction (your walls are see-through) → the condition and the question (to Wi-Fi; how does Wi-Fi go through walls?) → what it is (radio, 2.4 billion waves a second) → the proof (a government lab: most materials let it through) → the turn (but the higher 5 GHz band loses more) → metal worst → water soaks it up, even a fish tank → **hero twist: your microwave heats water at nearly that frequency, SAME BAND** → the tip for the viewer's home (central, off the floor, in the open) → the question that loops into line 1.

**Native move for this film: the ribbon narrowing.** The signal is a folded paper strip; every material it passes leaves it a little narrower; frequency is literally the fold count. It reads hand-made and it's true to the physics in kind (not to scale).

**Main search phrase: `Wi-Fi through walls`** (spoken inside `ask`, ≈ 3–5 s: "how does Wi-Fi go through walls"). Show **HOW DOES WI-FI GO THROUGH WALLS?** in the upper third from ≈ 2.0 s and in the caption's first line. Secondary phrases for the SEO agent (volumes not checked): `2.4 vs 5 GHz`, `where to put your router`, `best place for wifi router`, `why is my wifi slow`, `does a microwave interfere with wifi`.

**DM-able moment:** "Your microwave oven runs on the same band as your Wi-Fi" (the SAME BAND stamp). Second: "your walls are see-through to Wi-Fi" (NIST's "most striking" finding).

**Proposed YouTube title (4–8 words, Title Case, one emoji):** **"How Wi-Fi Goes Through Your Walls 🤔"**. Alternatives: "Why Your Microwave and Wi-Fi Share a Band 😮", "Where to Put Your Router 🤔".

**Wording choices tied to sources**
- `hook` "Your walls are see-through." + chip TO WI-FI: NIST's "relative 'transparency'" (fact 3). "See-through" is the plain-word version of NIST's own word, conditioned on screen and in `ask`. Not "every wall": concrete and metal are the exceptions, and the film says so (`metal`).
- `radio` "about 2.4 billion times a second": fact 1 + band edges (fact 8). "About", because channels sit between 2.400 and 2.4835 GHz.
- `lab` "A government lab found most wall materials let it through.": NIST (a US Commerce Department lab), fact 3. On screen: **NIST, 1997 · TESTED 0.5–2 AND 3–8 GHZ** so nobody reads it as a 2.4 GHz measurement (NIST's two ranges skip 2–3 GHz; the trend holds across both).
- `band` "the faster 5 GHz band loses more through walls": NIST trend (fact 4) + ASUS (fact 5). "Faster": 5 GHz is the higher-speed band (Apple/ASUS treat it as the less crowded, quicker option); if the jury questions "faster", swap to "higher" in `say` only.
- `metal` "Metal blocks it worst.": Apple's "Very high interference potential: metal" (fact 6), the highest of its four levels.
- `water` "And water soaks it up, even a fish tank.": FCC's "efficiently transfers energy to water molecules" (fact 7) + Apple lists water as an interference material (fact 6). A fish tank is just a big box of water in the path. On screen beside the tank: **WATER: MEDIUM · APPLE**, so the size of the effect isn't overstated.
- `twist` "Your microwave heats water at nearly that frequency.": FCC: ovens at 2.45 GHz heat water (fact 7); "nearly that frequency" = 2.45 vs about 2.4. `SAME BAND` = fact 8.
- `tip` "central, off the floor, out in the open": TP-Link and ASUS (fact 9). On the floor plan, AWAY FROM THE MICROWAVE = TP-Link + Apple.
- `end` "Is yours hiding in a cabinet?": asks the viewer; TP-Link and ASUS both say a cabinet blocks the signal.

**Kept out on purpose**
- "2.45 GHz is water's resonant frequency" (a myth; the FCC only says microwave frequencies transfer energy to water efficiently).
- Any claim that Wi-Fi heats you or anything else (power is nothing like an oven's; not this film's idea).
- Specific NIST percentages (e.g. drywall dissipating 6 %): the report doesn't tie them to a Wi-Fi frequency in that passage, and the tested ranges skip 2–3 GHz. The qualitative finding is safe; the numbers aren't for 2.4 GHz.
- How much a fish tank actually costs you: authoritative sources only rate water "medium"; a search result from an aquarium retailer (youraquarium.co.uk, seen in search results only, not read or cited) reports an informal test with no speed change from a new tank. So "can soak it up", no size, no "kills your Wi-Fi".
- How much a running oven degrades Wi-Fi in numbers (no primary measurement read; FDA leakage limit is a safety rule, not an interference figure).
- 6 GHz / Wi-Fi 6E and 7, mesh systems, channels 1/6/11, antenna angles: each a second idea.

## What we learned from the reference videos (2026-10-24)

**Danny Why, "The Easiest Way To Get Monetized on YouTube"** (QH4A3l6ht_A, faceless-channel creator).
- YouTube now judges each video on its own, much like TikTok. It shows a video to a small group, then to wider groups while each group keeps watching. Subscribers barely carry views (he has 200k and gets about 1k views in the first hours). So every upload has to win with strangers.
- Aim for consistency, not virality: every video should land in the same range (about 10k), rather than one hit followed by nothing.
- A video has two jobs: get the click (idea + title + thumbnail), then keep the viewer watching. A great package on a weak video fails, and so does the reverse. The video craft matters most.
- The hook is the tutorial. It's short and says why to stay. Long, vague intros are the most common mistake.
- **Give the viewer several quests.** Don't make one promise and pay it off only at the end. Run a chain of small problem → payoff loops, each one closed before the next opens, all inside the big promise.
- Work from a system (we have one: the factory and this playbook).
- His sponsor segment pushes an AI animation generator. That's exactly the look we avoid, because the channel must not look like AI.

**George Alexander, "If nobody watches your videos, do this."** (-Fr-TTxWIFs, about 5:17).
- **Cold open with no talking for about 20 s:** a pure visual story of the viewer's own situation (staring at the ceiling, a 0-views graph, typing "why am I getting no views"). Sound carries the mood: a ticking clock, frantic strings, then a hard cut to a calm cello on the first spoken line.
- **Named chapter cards:** "part 1 · the Switch", "part 2 · the Story", "part 3 · Finding your North Star". Each card is a plain full-frame card with a heavy mechanical clunk, and lasts 2–3 s.
- **One quotable line per chapter, built physically on screen.** Hands arrange cut-out words on a cutting mat to spell "Create like an artist but study like a scientist".
- **Split-screen contrast:** the same man twice in the same situation, discouraged versus curious. Counters over each half diverge (7 versus tens of thousands).
- Every scene is 2–5 s and the camera is static. The energy comes from cuts and sound, not camera moves.
- Mindset: each upload is an experiment testing an idea, a title, a thumbnail or a style. Read the result as data.
- The ending names the next video aloud ("click this video just up here") and holds an end card for 13 s.

## Checklist for the next episode

1. **Promise within 5 s.** The cables hook reached "every cable snaps" at 15 s. Next time the big "what if" lands by 0:05, and the escalation question follows by 0:10.
2. **Quests:** 4–6 chapters. Each opens a spoken question ("So what breaks first?") and closes it with a visible payoff (a number, a reveal, a consequence) before the next opens. Our time cards already work as chapter cards, so keep the slam and make each one ask its question.
3. **One quotable line per chapter,** built on screen by the stickman: he carries, stacks or drops the words. Use it for the title-worthy sentence of the chapter.
4. **At least one split-screen contrast** per episode (two "yous", two cities, with and without), with diverging counters.
5. **Sound tells the mood.** For YouTube long-form, consider a quiet music bed that changes per chapter (TikTok is the only place Isac adds sounds himself). Until he decides, keep voice and foley only, but make a hard sound switch at the hook-to-story turn.
6. **Packaging for strangers:** a thumbnail with 2–4 huge words and one clear image or graph with an arrow; the title is the "what if" question. Write 3 title and thumbnail pairs before building the film.
7. **End:** say the next video aloud and point to it; the end card holds for 10 s or more.
8. **Read the data 48 h after upload.** Record CTR, average view duration, the first big retention dip (timecode and what was on screen) and views versus our range in `factory/LESSONS.md`, then change one thing next time.

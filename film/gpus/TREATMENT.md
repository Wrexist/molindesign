# GPUs: why AI made Nvidia so rich

A vertical (1080×1920) explainer for TikTok, Reels and Shorts, written in code. Factory #12 on Tech, explained, after `film/password/` and `film/temperature/` (both parked at 7.2–7.5). One hard idea: **Nvidia's chips were built to paint game pixels all at once, and training AI turned out to be the same kind of work, billions of small multiply-adds at once; so the gaming-chip company now makes over 90 % of its sales from data centers.** Facts and sources: `BRIEF.md`.

Built to every LESSONS line before the first render (BRIEF §0): no title card. Frame 0 is one huge cream adding-machine tape hanging from a paper printer, its total printed in coral: **$96 / BILLION**, with **3 MONTHS** hand-lettered under a ruled line: four words, counted on the rendered frame, nothing else readable. The tape feeds down and the pencil underline draws by 0.3 s. The condition comes in at 1.5 s under the tape, never on it: NVIDIA SALES (REVENUE) / QUARTER ENDED JULY 26, 2026. The search phrase WHY IS NVIDIA SO RICH? is the headline on "So why".

## Three structures we considered

1. **Mechanism first** ("A CPU has 10 cores, a GPU 21,760"). Rejected: an abstract open, the worst-retaining kind (stats 2026-10-24), and the money stake would arrive too late.
2. **The 2012 story first** ("Two gaming cards started the AI boom"). Rejected for truth: no primary source says "started the boom" (BRIEF §2).
3. **$96 billion in three months → why so rich → over 90 % from data centers → but it started in games → Toronto 2012, two $499 gaming cards win by a mile → here's why: multiply, then add → laptop 10 big cores vs gaming card 21,760 tiny ones → all at once (hero) → made for game pixels, now trains AI → got one at home?** (chosen). A stake, a contradiction, a true story, one mechanism shown by one prop, a payoff that re-reads the same prop, and an end the picture doesn't answer.

## The native move: the stamp sheet

Work is ink going onto paper cells. A cream sheet (760 px wide) ruled into exactly 64 × 34 = 2,176 cells, each with a hand-drawn ×+ mark, labelled 1 CELL = 10 CORES (OUR MATH), so the sheet is the RTX 5090's 21,760 cores to scale. The laptop's CPU is a small rubber stamp that inks one cell per press. The gaming card is a carved print block: it comes in face up so the knife-cut grid can be read, turns over, hovers, and on "once" (in the pause after the word, with the film's one heavy thump) slams once and lifts away with all 2,176 cells inked on the same frame, no ripple. The film's only coral stamp, ALL AT ONCE, lands under the sheet, not on it. On "Made for game pixels" the same inked cells re-ink as a game frame; on "Now it trains AI" they flip back to multiply-add marks.

**Scene cuts** use the house torn-paper edge rising from the bottom along one axis, with the next scene already drawn under it. Within a scene, objects move in and out; no screen is replaced by an opacity crossfade. Sources of a scene appear only after the tear has covered the previous scene.

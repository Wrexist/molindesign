```
Does dark mode save battery? Not always. It depends on your screen and on your brightness. On an OLED screen every pixel is its own light, and a black pixel switches off, so dark mode can cut power. On an LCD screen a backlight sits behind every pixel and stays on, even for black, so dark mode can't switch it off. Try it: turn off the lights and open a black photo. If the black glows gray, it's an LCD. If it's pitch black, it's OLED. And even on OLED, the saving at indoor brightness is small; it gets big in bright sun.

The study. Purdue University news release, July 28, 2021: "At 30%-50% brightness, Purdue researchers found that switching from light mode to dark mode saves only 3%-9% power on average for several different OLED smartphones. This percentage is so small that most users wouldn't notice the slightly longer battery life." And: "Many people use their phone's default auto-brightness setting, which tends to keep brightness levels around 30%-40% most of the time when indoors." At full brightness: "The Purdue study found that switching from light mode to dark mode at 100% brightness saves an average of 39%-47% battery power." The rule of thumb: "But the higher the brightness when switching from light mode to dark mode, the higher the energy savings." The paper: Pranab Dash and Y. Charlie Hu, "How much battery does dark mode save? An accurate OLED display power profiler for modern smartphones," MobiSys 2021.

Why OLED and LCD differ. Purdue: "Smartphones that came out after 2017 likely have an OLED (organic light-emitting diode) screen. Because this type of screen doesn't have a backlight like the LCD (liquid crystal display) screens of older phones, the screen will draw less power when displaying dark-colored pixels." Samsung Display, as reported by Asia Economy on May 6, 2021: "each pixel in OLED emits light by itself and expresses black by turning off pixels" and "LCD backlights are always on even when displaying black".

Which iPhones. Apple's specs list iPhone 11 with a "Liquid Retina HD display" (an LCD) at "1400:1 contrast ratio (typical)", and iPhone 17 with a "Super Retina XDR display" (OLED) at "2,000,000:1 contrast ratio (typical)". On Apple's compare page, current models have Super Retina XDR (OLED) displays.

What Google says. Android Developers, "Implement dark theme": dark theme "Reduces power usage by a significant amount, depending on the device's screen technology."

So, does your black screen glow? Yes or no.

Fine print: Purdue tested 4 Android OLED phones (Pixel 2, Moto Z3, Pixel 4 and Pixel 5) running 6 Google apps, 60 seconds each; it did not test iPhones. The percentages are power draw, not extra hours of battery life, and your own saving depends on your phone, apps and brightness. LCD screens behave differently: the backlight stays on, so dark mode can't switch it off. The glow test is for phone screens; some laptops, tablets and TVs dim their backlight in zones and can look black too.
```

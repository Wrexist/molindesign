## 1. Topic and one hard idea

**Studies found no sign that apps send your audio out, and the best-known "we listen to phones for ads" pitch, per the FTC, used no voice data at all; it resold email lists from data brokers. That kind of data (searches, apps, location, broker lists) is what ads run on.**

**Backlog hook kept, shortened.** "Your phone probably isn't listening for ads." is 7 words; the rule is 6. Spoken hook: **"Your phone probably isn't listening."** (5), with **FOR ADS?** on frame 0 so the claim is scoped to ads from the first frame. "Probably" stays: the evidence is one 2018 Android study plus an FTC case, not proof that no app anywhere does it (see §5).

## 2. Hook options

1. **"Your phone probably isn't listening."** (chosen, 5 words) Turns the most-held belief about the phone in their hand; hedged; loops from "Yes or no." because the end question asks them to check whether it is.
2. "The listening ad company wasn't listening." (6, rejected) The best twist, but on frame 0 it needs context the viewer doesn't have yet, and it names a company story before the stake.
3. "Look at the top of your screen." (7, rejected) A real test, but too long and it gives the do-this away before the story.

## 5. Open questions and truth risks for the director

1. **The hook is a hedge, and frame 0 must scope it.** "Your phone probably isn't listening." is true of ad targeting as studied (facts 1–2, 5–7), not of the wake word. **FOR ADS?** on frame 0 and **PROBABLY NOT** by ~0.9 s carry that; `wake` then says out loud that it does listen for Hey Siri. If a jury still reads it as "never listens", change the frame-0 tag to **PROBABLY NOT FOR ADS**.
2. **Copy hedge words onto every number** (LESSONS darkmode): 17,000 always with **ANDROID APPS · IN THEIR TESTS · 2018**; "no voice data" always with **FTC SAYS / FTC COMPLAINT**; $880,000 (if shown) always **TO REPAY ITS CUSTOMERS**.
3. **No ✓ chips, no 0 % or "0 LEAKS" chips** (LESSONS airplanemode). The study result is a quote, not a tick.
4. **No labels that contradict what's shown.** **AS PITCHED** must sit on the sound-wave strips the whole time they move toward the box and leave with them at the tear; never let the strips sit inside the open box after `twist`.
5. **Re-created screens show no ON switches.** Settings rows end in chevrons; no toggles anywhere, or the end question gets answered.
6. **Don't show accused names.** CMG's pitch named big platforms as partners and data sources (FTC ¶7, 404 Media); none of those names or logos go on screen.
7. **Planned silences.** 0.5 s after `hook` (tape rip), 0.6 s after `dip` (dry thump, lamp), 0.5 s after `twist` (panel rip), 1.2 s after `lists` (one paper slap + summary + screenshot hold; one dry hit only, no ringing), 0.45 s after `dot` (soft pop).

# Treatment · Is your phone listening for ads?

Factory #33 (`phonelistening`), the director's plan. Script and sources: `BRIEF.md`, `vo/script.json` (Ming US_James, seed 7, no music). Engine, gates and sound come from `../overnightcharge/` (its `CLIP_RUN` and `LABELS_RUN` checks, safe box, quote strips, peel/label/hold patterns, captions, SFX gate). The look is this film's own: a phone and one cut-paper "Active Listening" box, all paper on dark slate.

## Two benchmarks
- **Learn from `overnightcharge` (#32, r2 7.3):** one hero object with a physical fail and a physical pass, every number label peeled before the picture moves past it, a drawn summary on the hero hold that becomes the poster, settings paths ending in neutral chevrons. **Don't take:** its locked bedroom; and its coral "?" on frame 0 (coral is now only the box's fail lamp, the payoff underline at the peak, and YES / NO).
- **Learn from `incognito` (Nov 2, 1,281 views):** the hook is the source's own idea, and the hero, the re-created screen and the settlement all explain one mechanism (here: the ads ran on data, not your voice). **Don't take:** a long text quote as the hero; the hero here is a box you watch get torn open.

## Three structures
1. **The box on the cord (chosen).** The phone's mic is plugged by a paper cord into a big black "Active Listening" box. The box lights coral when the ad seller pitches it (fail); an FTC tab tears its front off and there is no microphone inside, only folded email lists (pass); the lists spill across the floor and the summary is drawn over them (hero hold, poster). The cut cord's loose end then points up at the status-bar dot.
2. **A courtroom of claims.** The rumour, the study, Siri, the pitch and the FTC as five witnesses on a stand. Clear, but five talking cards is an infographic, not an object.
3. **A day of ads.** A timeline of one person's day with ads popping up from searches and location. True, but it has no twist: the CMG pitch, the belief's best evidence, would be a side note.

## The native move: the cord
One paper cord from the phone's mic. It plugs into the box at the dip (sound-wave strips ride along it, tagged AS PITCHED), it is cut by the FTC tear (a frayed stub inside, no mic), and its loose end becomes the pointer that leads to the status-bar dot.

## Rules for this film
- Frame 0: three words (LISTENING FOR ADS?), the phone (≈ 800 px tall) gliding 340 px in 0.55 s, a paper ear taped over its mic, its status-bar corner under a paper "?" flap. PROBABLY NOT (52 px) drops in on "probably" and holds to 4.1 s. The end card is the same picture, so the loop has no bump.
- 17,000 only as 17,000 ANDROID APPS with IN THEIR TESTS · 2018 in the same plate. The study is a quote ("NO AUDIO LEAKS AT ALL"), never a tick or a 0.
- FTC claims are attributed: FTC SAYS / FTC COMPLAINT. The pitch line is CMG's own website text as quoted by the FTC, tagged RE-CREATED. No platform named in CMG's pitch appears anywhere.
- AS PITCHED rides on the sound-wave strips and leaves with them when they fall.
- The iPhone mic dot is drawn in Apple's own orange (#ff9f0a) and labelled ORANGE = MIC (iPHONE); house coral is #ff5b3d and only on the lamp, the payoff underline and YES / NO.
- Settings: RE-CREATED, generic rows, neutral chevrons, no switches, MENU NAMES VARY.
- Props stay whole inside x 92–928, y 302–1428; backgrounds bleed full width, dark.
- SFX: dry hits only, in the written voice gaps: tape peel (hook gap), lamp thump (dip gap), panel tear (twist gap), sheet slap (lists hold, one hit), dot pop (dot gap).

Is your phone listening to you for ads? Probably not. A 2018 study of 17,000+ Android apps found no audio leaks, and the best-known "we listen" ad pitch used no voice data at all, the FTC says. It resold email lists from data brokers.

The study. "Panoptispy" (PETS 2018): "We did not find any true positive audio files in our extracted dataset, i.e., no apps appeared to exfiltrate audio in our tests." Northeastern quotes co-author David Choffnes: "There were no audio leaks at all—not a single app activated the microphone."

The wake word. Apple, on Siri: "any audio that does not contain the trigger keywords is discarded." Google Assistant Help: "When in standby mode, it won't send what you're saying to Google servers or anyone else."

The pitch. The FTC's complaint against Cox Media Group quotes its website: "Creepy? Sure. Great for marketing? Definitely." The FTC alleges: "In fact, Respondent's Active Listening service did not collect or use voice data in any manner." FTC, May 21, 2026: it consisted of "reselling—at a significant markup—email lists obtained from other data brokers." FTC, Aug 27, 2026, finalizing the orders: "the marketing service wasn't based on voice data." These are the FTC's allegations and a settlement, not a court finding.

Where ads come from. Google My Ad Center Help lists "things you've looked for on Google Search", "apps you've installed on your Android device" and "your general area while using Google Services". An FTC staff report found big social media and streaming companies kept data "including information from data brokers".

The dot. Apple: "An orange indicator means the microphone is being used by an app on your iPhone." Android Help: "When apps use your camera or mic, a green indicator will show at the top right corner of your screen."

How to check yours. Look at the top of your screen: on iPhone, orange = mic, green = camera (or camera and mic); on Android, a green indicator, which you can swipe down and tap to see the app. To see which apps can use your mic: iPhone, Settings › Privacy & Security › Microphone. Android 12 and later, Settings › Security and privacy › Privacy dashboard. Menu names vary.

Fine print: This is one 2018 study of Android apps; its authors note an app could turn audio into "a text transcript" they wouldn't detect, and they missed events "in apps that require login". So: probably, not never. The wake word is checked on the phone, but after it, your request goes to the company's servers. The screens are re-created; no real interface or logo is shown.

## 1. Topic and one hard idea

**Each extra random lowercase letter multiplies the possible passwords by 26, so on Hive Systems' 2026 rig eight random letters fall in about two weeks while twenty would take over four quadrillion years (our math at Hive's speed). But none of that counts if the password is reused or made of real words: Hive's own second table says "Instantly" at every length. So go long, and turn on two-step login so a stolen password isn't enough.**

Story order (escalating consequences, the what-if shape): the viewer's own length (frame 0 test) → 8 letters = 2 weeks (threat) → the search question → **what if 20?** → the dial: ×26 per letter → 9 = 1 year → 12 = 21,000 years → 18 = 6 trillion, the table ends → **hero: 20 = over 4 quadrillion years, our math** → **dip: reused or real words? Instantly** → NIST: 15 if it's the only step → turn on two-step → question about your email → loop to frame 0.

**Native move: the typed tile drop.** One paper tile per letter falls into its cell with a knock; the tag above re-stamps only when the count reaches a row the table has. On `dip` the tiles flip over to show real words (made-up example: **sunnybeach2024** style, tagged EXAMPLE) and the tag snaps straight to INSTANTLY.

**Main search phrase: `how long should your password be`** (spoken word for word in `ask`, ≈ 2.4–4.6 s Ming). Show **HOW LONG SHOULD YOUR PASSWORD BE?** in the upper third from the start of `ask` and in the caption's first line. Secondary (volumes not checked): `how long to crack a password`, `password crack time 2026`, `hive systems password table 2026`, `is a 20 character password safe`, `NIST password length 15`, `turn on two step verification`.

**DM-able moment:** "Eight random letters? Two weeks. Twenty? Over four quadrillion years." Second: the INSTANTLY flip (send it to the friend who reuses one password everywhere).

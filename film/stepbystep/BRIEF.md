## 1. Topic and one hard idea

**A chatbot guesses each word from the text in front of it, so when "Let's think step by step" makes it write the steps first, its answer is guessed with those steps in view. In a 2022 test that lifted one model from 17.7% to 78.7% on math word problems; steps written after the answer did no better, and today's reasoning models write the steps on their own.**

Story order: the source's five words (hook) → the dated, scoped number → the question → the paper's puzzle (viewer tries it) → the model said 8 → with the five words it wrote steps, then 4 → mechanism: each step goes back in → **hero: its answer is guessed with those steps in view** (stamp + hold) → proof by order swap (Wei) → honest turn (reasoning models do it themselves) → question that loops into line 1.

**Native move for this film: the answer strip.** A paper receipt that prints step tabs and then the answer, with the tabs fed back into the box. It *is* the mechanism (the paper's two-stage pipeline), it reads with the sound off, and the `after` beat re-uses it by swapping the order.

**Main search phrase: `think step by step`** (spoken in the hook, 0.2–1.9 s). Show **"LET'S THINK STEP BY STEP"** as the flipped tiles from ≈ 0.6 s and put it in the caption's first line. Secondary (volumes not checked): `chain of thought prompting`, `chatgpt prompt tips`, `think step by step prompt`, `does think step by step still work`, `reasoning models`, `prompt engineering`.

**DM-able moment:** the puzzle. "The AI said 8. It's 4." People send a puzzle a machine missed. Second: "Five words took it from 18 % to 79 %." Third (pinned comment): in the same paper, "Don't think. Just feel." scored 18.8 %, about the same as no prompt at all (17.7 %).

**Proposed YouTube title:** **"Why 'Think Step By Step' Works On AI 🤔"**. Alternatives: "Five Words That Fixed AI's Math 🤔" (careful: "fixed" overclaims; prefer "helped"), "The Puzzle ChatGPT Got Wrong 🤔" (no: the paper tested GPT-3 via the API, not ChatGPT).

## 2. Hook options

1. **"Let's think step by step."** (chosen; 5 words; frame 0 `5 WORDS`) The source's own sentence, word for word, which is what passed incognito and voiceclone. It is also the search phrase, spoken at 0.2 s. It claims nothing, so it can't overclaim; the number arrives in line 2 with its scope.
2. "Five words that make AI smarter." (backlog hook, rejected) "Smarter" overclaims: the paper measured accuracy on specific reasoning tests with specific 2022 models, found no gain on its commonsense tests ("Zero-shot-CoT does not provide performance gains"), and found it "not effective" on smaller models. "Make AI" also implies every AI today, which OpenAI's own guide contradicts for reasoning models.
3. "The AI said 8. It's 4." (6 words, rejected as the hook, kept as the body's stake) A strong puzzle, but on frame 0 it would show the answer before the viewer tries (strawberry r1), and "the AI" without the five words doesn't tell the viewer what the film is about in the first 2 s.

- `end` asks the viewer; no claim.

**Kept out on purpose**
- "Smarter", "think", "understands": the paper measures test accuracy. "Makes AI think" is not claimed; the five words are a prompt, and the film calls the output "steps".
- The backlog angle's "It can only think out loud": dropped. Models compute a lot inside each word before writing it (Wei et al. itself notes "fully characterizing a model's computations that support an answer remains an open question", and the `math` film already used Anthropic's 2025 interpretability work showing internal multi-step work). The film keeps only what the papers show: written steps become input, and the answer is generated after them.
- "Every chatbot / ChatGPT gets this wrong today": the puzzle and numbers are from GPT-3 text-davinci-002 in 2022. Never say ChatGPT gets 8 now.
- Few-shot CoT, PaLM, GSM8K's 10.4 → 40.7, self-consistency, model-size curves: true, a second idea each.
- "It costs more": true (fact 7, time and tokens), but a second idea; pinned comment at most.
- Whether a reasoning model's visible "thinking" is its real reasoning (faithfulness): an open research question; out.
- OpenAI's o1 page ("learning to reason with LLMs") returned 403 today, so nothing from it is quoted.

**Main search phrase:** `your connection is not private` (spoken in `ask`, about 3.5–5 s; on screen by ~1.5 s as a typed search bar **YOUR CONNECTION IS NOT PRIVATE**; SEO captions it, plus "what does your connection is not private mean").

## 1. Topic and one hard idea

**Every secure site shows your browser a certificate, a dated ID card; the browser checks it (including its dates against your device's clock), and when it can't verify it, it stops before anything is sent and shows "Your connection is not private". Usually the cause is boring (your clock, a Wi-Fi sign-in page, the site's own expired or misconfigured certificate), but sometimes someone is pretending to be the site, and the screen can't promise which, so check the boring causes and never click through on a bank.**

**Backlog hook kept, tightened.** "This warning doesn't mean you were hacked." is 7 words; "This warning doesn't mean you're hacked." is 6. Is it true? The warning is shown *instead of* the page: Chrome's own text says it "stopped the connection before any data was exchanged" (fact 3) and Google's own study says "a wide range of non-attack circumstances trigger hundreds of millions of spurious browser warnings per month" (fact 7). It is not "always harmless": Chrome's own page says "Attackers might be trying to steal your information" (fact 2) and Chrome's details text names "an attacker … trying to pretend to be" the site as one cause (fact 3). So the hook says what the warning *is not* (proof you were hacked), never that it's safe, and `dip` states the real-attack case 26 s in, in Chrome's own verb ("pretend").

## 2. Hook options

1. **"This warning doesn't mean you're hacked."** (chosen, 6 words) The belief turned over, incognito shape ("doesn't make you invisible"), with the warning on frame 0 as "this". Scoped in the film by `dip`. Loops: "Have you ever clicked through? Yes or no." → "This warning doesn't mean you're hacked."
2. "Your clock can break every website." (6 words, rejected as the hook) The most surprising fact, but it overclaims: it breaks HTTPS sites only when far enough off, and Chrome now often shows a separate "Your clock is behind" page instead (fact 4). Kept as the DM-able middle.
3. "Check your clock before you panic." (6 words, rejected) A good takeaway, but it's an instruction with no belief to break and no search phrase.

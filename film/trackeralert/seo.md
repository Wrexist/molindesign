**Ready replies for the first hour:**
- **If:** I got the alert but I can't find the tracker.
  **Reply:** Apple says to look in "an item that you don't check often, like a jacket pocket, the outer compartment of a bag, or your vehicle." Tap the alert and play a sound. If you can't find it and feel unsafe, Apple and Google both say: go to a safe public location and contact law enforcement.
- **If:** Play Sound is grayed out. Does that mean I'm fine?
  **Reply:** Not necessarily. Apple: "the item might not be with you anymore or it's within range of its owner. Or if it was with you overnight, its identifier might have changed."
- **If:** What about Airplane Mode?
  **Reply:** Same answer. Google: if you "turn on Airplane Mode, your phone won't prevent the owner of the tracker or device from finding the location of the tracker." And on iPhone, Apple says that in Airplane Mode "you won't receive tracking notifications."
- **If:** Will the owner know I played the sound?
  **Reply:** Google says: "You won't notify the owner if you play the sound." That's Google's page; Apple's page doesn't say either way.
- **If:** It went off when I was with my friend.
  **Reply:** That happens. Google: "You may get these notifications if you borrow an item or if you're traveling with someone who has a tracker with them." Apple says alerts can come from "borrowed AirPods, or AirPods inadvertently left behind by a friend or family member."
- **If:** Should I just turn the tracker off?
  **Reply:** Only if it's safe. Google: "keep it turned on if you're concerned that turning it off could be unsafe." Some trackers "may be factory reset" when turned off, and then "Law enforcement can't get additional information about these trackers". Apple says law enforcement can work with Apple and you might need to provide the item and its serial number.

# Why is it called Grand Theft Auto? (Delta, pillar 3: GTA history)

Slug `gta-name` · researched 2026-11-17 · channel **Delta** (never Tech, explained). American English, US audience, own voice (Ming US_James, seed 7), own hand-built paper graphics, **no music**, no Rockstar footage, logo, key art or box art, no "AI" anywhere. GTA 6 launches 2026-11-19; nothing in the film says "coming", "isn't out yet" or "in X days".

**Overlap with gta-origin (read first).** gta-origin is "GTA was almost canceled" (the crashes, the cop bug, Penn's account). This film shares exactly one line with it: Race'n'Chase was the first name. It never mentions the bug, the near-cancel, the crashes or Gary Penn.

**Truth-proofing.**
- "Grand theft auto" is the everyday name for a legal charge, not the statute's literal wording. California's statute says grand theft is theft "when the property taken is ... an automobile" (PC 487(d)(1)); lawyers call that offense "grand theft auto". The voice says "California's law spells it out: an automobile" and "Lawyers call that grand theft auto", and the re-created statute page prints only the statute's own words.
- **Value is left out.** Cornell's Wex says a car counts "even if that property is not worth the minimum amount", while Shouse Law Group lists "worth more than $950" as an element. The sources disagree, so no line or label says "any car" or gives a dollar figure.
- **Felony is left out.** In California grand theft can be charged as a misdemeanor or a felony (a "wobbler", per the law-firm pages), so nothing says FELONY.
- **Who chose the name is unknown.** Dave Jones (DMA Design's founder) says the name "was changed soon after" BMG agreed to publish. He doesn't say who chose it. Steve Hammond (DMA) writes: "So who had the idea for GTA? ... I wasn't in the meeting and any recorded details are currently unknown to me." A Wikipedia mirror credits Dan and Sam Houser, unsourced: **left out**. The film never names who picked it and never says why.
- "GTA is a real crime" is the hook's shorthand for "grand theft auto is a real charge"; line 2 says what it means at once.

## 1. One hard idea
**"Grand Theft Auto" is a real US legal charge (stealing a car is grand theft under California's law), and the game only got that name after a publisher came on board; its makers had called it Race'n'Chase.** One hero object: **the typewriter and its charge sheet.**

DM-able moment: the strike-through. The typewriter strikes RACE'N'CHASE through in red and types GRAND THEFT AUTO under it.

## 2. Hook options
1. **"GTA is a real crime."** **Chosen.** 5 words, GTA first spoken (0.2 s) and first on screen. Breaks a belief about a name they say every day.
2. "Grand Theft Auto is a crime you can be charged with." True, too long for frame 0.
3. "GTA was almost called Race'n'Chase." Accurate, but it's gta-origin's territory and gives the twist away.

Frame 0: caption `GTA IS A REAL CRIME` (5 words). The sheet in the typewriter is blank (lines only, no words); the evidence bag with a paper car glides ≥ 300 px in the first second.

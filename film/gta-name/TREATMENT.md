# Treatment: "Why Is It Called Grand Theft Auto?" (Delta, gta-name)

One hard idea: **"Grand Theft Auto" is a real US legal charge, and the game only took that name after a publisher came on board; its makers had called it Race'n'Chase.** One hero object: **the typewriter and its charge sheet.** Hero sounds: the key clacks and carriage returns in the pauses, the red-pencil scratch, the margin bell, the strike-through run of keys.

## Three structures
1. **One charge sheet in a typewriter (chosen).** The legal meaning and the name history are typed onto the same sheet, so the film's twist (the name was struck out and retyped) happens in the medium itself.
2. A police evidence board with string. Rejected: crime-show cliché, and the string-board reads as conspiracy (implies a hidden cause).
3. A dictionary page flipping to the entry. Rejected: no native event for "the name changed".

## Native move (round 2)
The platen roll: each new claim rolls the sheet up one line while the carriage creeps and slides back. Three sheets go through the machine: sheet A (the law) is ripped out on "at first", sheet B (the name) is struck and rolled out before the vote, sheet C is blank (frame 0 and the end). The camera breaks the centred frame twice: it travels down the desk to a full-width statute page (law) and to the opened DMA Design folder (race/pub), then comes back up and pushes in to 1.3x for the strike-through, which is held clean (struck RACE'N'CHASE, red GRAND THEFT AUTO under it) for about 1.4 s before the GTA 6 plate. That hold is the poster.

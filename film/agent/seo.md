Nobody touched the mouse. So what is an AI agent, actually?

Inside is the same kind of next-word guesser a chatbot uses. The difference: instead of guessing the next word, it guesses the next action, like a click on a button. But the model can't click anything itself. Its action comes out as text, and other software does the click, then hands it back a screenshot. Google's docs put it plainly: "The model doesn't execute the function itself." Anthropic's computer use docs say "your application runs every call in an environment you control."

Then it looks at the new screenshot and guesses again. Think, act, look, again and again. Anthropic describes agents as "typically just LLMs using tools based on environmental feedback in a loop." Researchers named the pattern ReAct in 2022: "multiple thought-action-observation steps."

But every step is still a guess. In October 2024, while Anthropic was recording demos for its computer use launch, Claude "suddenly took a break from our coding demo and began to peruse photos of Yellowstone National Park."

That's why the companies building agents say it should stop and ask you before it buys or sends. Google says user confirmation "is required for certain actions such as making a purchase." OpenAI's docs say "Keep users in control of purchases, data transmission, destructive changes, and other actions that are hard to reverse."

So, would you let it click send? Yes or no.

Fine print: "Nobody touched the mouse" describes our illustration, not one logged real run; the email, send button, screens and click coordinates in the video are hand-made re-creations, and no real app is shown. "Meant to ask you" is the builders' guidance, not a guarantee that every agent does; how often an agent asks first depends on the product and its settings.

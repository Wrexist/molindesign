# Brief · The whole history of Nvidia

Long-form episode 3 of Tech, explained on YouTube (16:9, about 10 minutes), and the first of the weekly "whole history of…" series Isac asked for on 2026-10-28, modelled on Built On You's "The ENTIRE History of Amazon in 20 Minutes" (youtu.be/jOo8wSIeGus). Same stickman rig and render contract as `film/cables/` and `film/solar/`, Chatterbox voice (Isac's long-form pick on 2026-10-24), voice and foley only.

**The one hard idea:** Nvidia isn't an AI company that used to make gaming chips. It's a gaming company whose math turned out to be the math of AI. The "stadium of kids" (a GPU) against "a few brilliant professors" (a CPU) is the picture that carries it, and it comes back in parts 4, 5 and the ending.

## What we took from the Amazon reference (subtitles read 2026-10-28)
- Cold open on a concrete present-day moment (the tracking-page dot), then the thesis in one reversal ("not a store that has warehouses, a warehouse that grew a store"), then scale numbers, then "here's what should be impossible", then three questions, then a teaser list of odd story beats, then the title.
- Planted callbacks ("Remember that bell / that lawyer / that $18 share") paid off much later. Ours: Denny's, the toilets, thirty days from dead, the $12 share, the stadium of kids, the student's house.
- One myth-bust ("You may have heard…"). Ours: "Nvidia just got lucky with AI."
- A fast montage of years near the end, then "which brings us to right now" with this month's numbers, then "so why does any of this matter", an ending that rhymes with the opening, and a call to action.
- Don't take: 20 minutes and a slow founder childhood. Ours is 10 minutes and reaches the founding by 2:40.

## Wording calls
- "Most of the world's AI chips": Nvidia's share of AI accelerators is usually put at 80–90%; we don't say a number.
- "The gaming company now mostly sells to AI" stands on Data Center = 92.5% of revenue.
- Valentine's line is told as the book tells it; the card says "as told in *The Nvidia Way*".
- People are drawn as stickmen with one costume cue each (Jensen's leather jacket). Only quotes from the table are spoken or shown as theirs.

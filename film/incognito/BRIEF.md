## 1. Topic and one hard idea

**Incognito only forgets on your device. Every page you open still travels across your network to the website, so the sites, your school or work network and your internet provider can still see you. Chrome's own incognito screen says so, and in a 2024 settlement Google agreed to scrub billions of records.**

Story order: the stake (incognito isn't invisible) → the question with the search phrase (what does incognito mode actually hide?) → the checkable proof (Chrome's own screen lists who still sees you) → the mechanism (it only forgets on your device; the page still crosses the network) → **hero: it forgets for you, not for them** (stamp + hold) → the dated stake (Brown v. Google settlement, 2024) → the question that loops into line 1.

**Native move for this film: the envelope on a string.** Every page is a sealed envelope that leaves your laptop and passes booths whose windows slide open. Hand-made, readable with the sound off, and literally the mechanism: the request leaves the device; incognito only cleans up what stayed behind.

**Main search phrase: `incognito mode`** (spoken in `ask`, ≈ 2.3–4.8 s chain). Show **WHAT DOES INCOGNITO MODE HIDE?** in the upper third from ≈ 2.0 s and in the caption's first line. Secondary phrases for the SEO agent (volumes not checked): `does incognito mode hide you`, `can my school see incognito`, `can wifi owner see incognito history`, `is incognito really private`, `google incognito lawsuit`.

**DM-able moment:** "Your school or work can still see your incognito tabs, and Chrome's own screen says so." Second: Google agreed in 2024 to scrub billions of private-browsing records.

**Proposed YouTube title (4–8 words, Title Case, one emoji):** **"What Incognito Mode Really Hides 🤔"**. Alternatives: "Who Can Still See Your Incognito Tabs 😱", "Incognito Isn't Invisible 🤔".

## 2. Hook options

1. **"Incognito doesn't make you invisible."** (chosen; 5 words; frame 0 `INCOGNITO ISN'T INVISIBLE`, 3 words) A contradiction the viewer feels (the name promises hiding) in Google's own words ("it doesn't make you invisible", Chrome Help, read today), so the hook and the proof are the same source. It sets up exactly the mechanism the body explains: hidden on the device, not on the network.
2. "Incognito mode doesn't hide you. Here's who still sees." (the backlog hook, rejected as written) 9 words, over the 6-word cap, and "doesn't hide you" is too absolute: it does hide your history from people who share the device. Its second half survives as `visible` + `network`.
3. "Your incognito tab lists who's watching." (6 words, rejected) True and checkable, but "watching" overstates "might still be visible to" / "may be able to observe", and it hands the answer (the list) to the hook.

- HTTPS/what exactly an ISP sees (domain vs page): no primary source read today; the envelope stays sealed.

Incognito doesn't make you invisible. So what does incognito mode actually hide?

It's printed on Chrome's own incognito screen. Under "Chrome won't save:" it lists "Your browsing history", "Cookies and site data" and "Information entered in forms". Under "Your activity might still be visible to:" it lists "Websites you visit", "Your employer or school" and "Your internet service provider".

Here's why. Incognito only forgets on your device. Google's Chrome Help page says: "While Incognito can help keep your browsing private on your device, it doesn't make you invisible." Every page you open still crosses your network to the site, and the same page says "organizations that manage your network, like your school, employer, or internet service provider, may be able to observe your activity in Incognito." It forgets for you. Not for them.

In a 2024 settlement of Brown v. Google, Google agreed to delete or remediate old private browsing data, which by "Google's current estimate" will impact "billions of event records". Google admits no wrongdoing: the agreement says "Google is not admitting any liability, fault, or violation of law."

So, did you think incognito hid you? Yes or no.

## 1. Topic and one hard idea

**Two things speed up battery wear: heat and long hours at full charge. Overnight, a phone with its charging setting on handles the hours itself (it can hold at 80 % and finish right before you usually unplug); you handle the heat.**

**Backlog hook changed.** "Charging overnight isn't what wears your battery out." overclaims against the makers' own pages: Google says 100 % "for a long time … can strain the battery long term" and "don't keep it charging more than needed"; Samsung US says "Unplug the charger after the device has reached a 100% charge to avoid a swollen battery" (facts 3, 5). Overnight charging *with the feature off* does add hours at 100 %. The new hook says what the phones actually do instead of acquitting the habit: **"Your phone doesn't charge all night."** (6 words). The body hedges it the way the sources do ("it can just stop", Apple: "in certain situations").

## 2. Hook options

1. **"Your phone doesn't charge all night."** (chosen, 6 words) Turns a belief about the thing on the viewer's nightstand; checkable tonight on the lock screen; loops from "Yes or no." Truth note in §5.1.
2. "Charging overnight isn't the problem." (5 words, rejected) The backlog's idea, shorter. Overclaims (Samsung US: unplug at 100 %; Google: don't keep it charging more than needed).
3. "Your pillow is cooking your battery." (6 words, rejected) Strong threat, but no source measures pillow temperature or pillow wear; Apple's pillow line is a skin-safety warning (fact 6). Kept as the `pillow` beat with careful words.

**Wording choices tied to sources**
- `hook` "doesn't charge all night": facts 1, 3, 4 (it delays past 80 / completes right before you unplug / stops at 80). Hedged in the body ("can just stop") and on screen (**WHEN THE SETTING IS ON**, see §5.1).
- `wear` "Two things speed up wear: heat, and hours at full charge." Apple: chemical age from factors "including temperature history and charging pattern"; Optimized Charging reduces wear by "reducing the time your iPhone spends fully charged"; Google: 100 % for a long time "can strain the battery long term". "Speed up", not "cause": we never say these are the only causes.
- `pillow` "that heat gets trapped": Google "Your phone warms up when it's plugged in" + Apple's "well-ventilated area" (fact 6). Our inference that a pillow traps heat; no number on screen.
- `dip` "heat can damage a battery for good": Apple "can permanently damage battery capacity" (above 95 °F ambient). Chip copies the hedge and the condition: **ABOVE 95°F, HEAT "CAN PERMANENTLY DAMAGE BATTERY CAPACITY" · APPLE**. Never a percentage of capacity lost.
- `open` "Charge it in the open, out of a hot case.": Apple fact 2 ("If you notice that your device gets hot when you charge it, take it out of its case first") and fact 6 ("well-ventilated area"). On screen: **IN THE OPEN · NOT UNDER A PILLOW** and **HOT? CASE OFF · APPLE**.
- `pause` "At 80 percent, it can just stop.": Apple "delay charging past 80 percent in certain situations"; Samsung Adaptive "stop charging at 80%"; Pixel "Limit to 80%" or Adaptive Charging. "Can" is the hedge; chip **80% CHARGE · "IN CERTAIN SITUATIONS" · APPLE**. The 80 is charge level, so the column says **80% CHARGE**, never a bare 80 (LESSONS battery r1).
- `learn` "It learned your routine.": Apple "learn your daily charging routine"; Samsung "Once your device learns your routine"; Google "about 14 days to learn your charging habits". Chip: **LEARNS FOR ABOUT 14 DAYS** (Apple "at least 14 days", Google "about 14 days").
- `full` "So it fills up right before you unplug.": Google "completes charging right before you typically unplug" / "charge to 100% one hour before you unplug"; Apple "aims to ensure that your iPhone is still fully charged when unplugged". Chip: **PIXEL: "100% ONE HOUR BEFORE YOU UNPLUG" · GOOGLE**.
- `check` "Look under Settings, then Battery.": every brand starts there. - `end` "Is yours set to wait? Yes or no.": a yes/no the viewer answers about their own phone; the picture must not hint at an answer.

**Kept out on purpose (truth risks)**
- **No percentage of capacity saved or lost.** No primary source gives one for optimized charging, pillows or overnight charging. Battery University's 80 %/96 % storage table is battery #8's and secondary; not used.
- **Never "never charge to 100 %".** Apple says the phone "will occasionally charge to 100 percent"; Google says a Pixel "needs to fully charge at every 10th cycle". The film tops off to 100 % on screen at `full`, on purpose.
- **Never "overnight charging is safe/harmless"** (Samsung US: unplug at 100 % to avoid a swollen battery; Google: don't keep it charging more than needed).
- **Pillow = battery damage is our inference.** Apple's pillow line is about skin and heat contact; Google says plugged-in phones warm up and heat can damage the battery. The voice says "heat gets trapped" and "heat can damage a battery", never "a pillow ruins your battery" or a temperature under a pillow.
- **The night's clock times are an example.** The 11 PM / 1 AM / 6:30 AM cards are illustration (tag **EXAMPLE NIGHT** ≥ 36 px); no source says when a phone hits 80.
- **The feature isn't guaranteed.** Apple: activates only when it predicts a long charge, needs 14 days and 9 charges of 5 h+; Pixel: "may turn on"; Samsung: "Once your device learns your routine". Hence "can just stop" and the end question.
- **Defaults.** Only Apple says "on by default". Don't say the Pixel or Samsung setting is on by default.
- **Heat-triggered 80 % cap** (Apple "Software may limit charging above 80%…", Pixel "Protecting your battery … 70%-80%") is a second idea; at most a small chip under `dip`, not voiced.
- **Fast charging, wireless charging, cheap chargers**: separate topics; cut.

## 5. Open questions and truth risks for the director

1. **The hook is a strong statement.** "Your phone doesn't charge all night" is true of the feature (facts 1, 3, 4) but not of every phone on every night (14-day learning, "in certain situations", "may turn on"). Show a small line under frame 0's caption by 2.5 s, or with the search bar: **WITH ITS CHARGING SETTING ON**. If the jury still docks Truth, swap the hook to "Your phone may not charge all night." (7 words) and keep everything else.
2. **Hedges on every number** (LESSONS darkmode): 80 % always with "IN CERTAIN SITUATIONS" or "CAN"; 95 °F always with "CAN PERMANENTLY DAMAGE"; 14 days always "ABOUT"; Pixel's "one hour" always credited to Pixel only.
3. **No ✓ chips, no 0 % chips** (LESSONS airplanemode). The do-this plate quotes the source verbs; no tick on IN THE OPEN.
4. **The column's 100 % must arrive at `full`, not earlier.** The peg holds at 80 through `learn`; the column never shows a % number it isn't at.
5. **Re-created screens.** The lock-screen "fully charged by" card and the settings screen are ours, generic, tagged RE-CREATED. Don't copy Apple's exact notification UI.
6. **Planned silences.** 0.5 s after `hook` (lamp click), 0.6 s after `dip` (dry thump), 0.45 s after `open` (case flap), 0.5 s after `pause` (peg clunk), 1.2 s after `full` (alarm + top slap + screenshot hold; one dry hit only, LESSONS darkmode: no ringing hit alone in a long gap, so the alarm bell is drawn shaking but its sound is a single short ring, or silent).
7. **Ming risks.** "11 at night" is spelled out in `say` ("Eleven at night"); "80 percent" as "eighty percent". `wear` has a colon list ("heat, and hours at full charge"): if Ming drops pitch at "heat," and reads it as two sentences, reword to "Heat, and hours at full charge, speed up wear." `full` and `end` both end on a stressed word; `end` uses `say` "Yes, or no?" like darkmode. "Pixel", "Samsung", "iPhone" are in `names` but not spoken.

9. **Packaging:** frame 0 ≤ 4 words (CHARGING ALL NIGHT? = 3). Check it on the render.

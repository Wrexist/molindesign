# Treatment · Does charging overnight ruin your battery?

Factory #32 (`overnightcharge`), the director's plan. Script and sources: `BRIEF.md`, `vo/script.json` (Ming US_James, seed 7). Engine, gates and sound come from `../darkmode/` (its `CLIP_RUN` and `LABELS_RUN` checks, safe box, quote strips, captions, rest-peg idea, SFX gate). The look is this film's own: one bedroom at night, cut paper.

## Two benchmarks
- **Learn from `darkmode` (#31, r2 7.4):** one physical set, a hero object that reacts all the time and gets one big physical event, a rest peg so the gauge never points at a number it doesn't claim, hedges copied onto every number. **Don't take:** its locked phone-left / meter-right layout (it read as a template for 20 s). Here the camera changes three times: bed close-up, top-down nightstand, wide room, then the settings screen.
- **Learn from `coldbattery` (#27, 1,216 views):** a phone myth with a check the viewer can run tonight. **Don't take:** a coral stamp over a drawn phone. The hero here is a cut-paper charge column (a tall glass on the nightstand, like a glass of water filling) with a brass peg at its 80 tick.

## Three structures
1. **One night on the nightstand.** Bed close-up: the phone slides onto the nightstand, the lamp cord is pulled, the room goes ink. Two culprits get pinned on: a paper HEAT thermometer and an HOURS AT 100% tag on the column. A pillow flops onto the phone, heat squiggles bounce back under it and the thermometer climbs into its coral bulb (fail). Top-down: a cut-paper hand pulls the phone into the open and the hot case drops off (pass). Wide room: the window's moon crosses the night, the fill rises and clunks onto the 80 peg (pass), sits there, and at dawn the peg swings aside and the fill slaps the top as the alarm buzzes (hero). Settings screen, then frame 0 with YES / NO.
2. **A split screen of two nights** (feature on / off) side by side. Clear, but both halves would be small and it says "off is bad", which the sources don't measure.
3. **A timeline chart** of charge against the clock. Accurate, but it is a chart, not an object, and it has no heat half.

**Chosen: 1.** The night is a story with a dip (the pillow) and a peak (the top-off), and every beat is a physical event on paper props.

## The native move: the night passes
One continuous sky in the wide room's window: the moon arcs left to right and the sky goes ink → navy → dawn peach as the clock chip in the window changes (11:00 PM, 1:00 AM, 4:00 AM, 6:30 AM, all tagged EXAMPLE NIGHT). The time jumps read without words.

## Rules for this film
- Frame 0: three words (CHARGING ALL NIGHT?, sticker type fitted with ≥ 28 px padding), the phone 740 px tall already gliding 300 px in 0.5 s, its screen lit with a halo and about 20 % more contrast until the lamp goes off, the fill rising with a glow. The column stands clear of the phone at the right (x 818–914, ticks showing) on frame 0 and at the end, so the loop matches. The hedge WITH ITS CHARGING SETTING ON (44 px) comes in on "doesn't" (0.6 s) and stays to 5.0 s.
- Nightstand line at y 1410 in every side view, so y 1330–1430 carries the props, not empty brown.
- The column shows no numbers until the wide room. There the fill is always moving until it rests on the brass peg at 80 (then it breathes 78–80, never over) and stays on the peg until "unplug" begins. 80% CHARGE · "…IN CERTAIN SITUATIONS" · APPLE sits beside it from the clunk and peels off at 26.3 s, a second before the fill leaves 80 (27.34 s). No 100 label appears before the fill reaches 100 on the slap (27.94 s).
- 95°F only with HEAT "CAN PERMANENTLY DAMAGE BATTERY CAPACITY" · APPLE. 14 days only as ABOUT 14 DAYS. "One hour before you unplug" only as Pixel, with "may turn on".
- Coral only on the thermometer's bulb and HEAT title at the low point, the column's top slap at the peak, frame 0's "?" and YES / NO.
- No ✓, no 0 %, no stock icons: the hand, pillow, moon, thermometer, flame and clock are cut paper. Generic phone and a generic re-created settings screen, tagged RE-CREATED; the settings paths end in a neutral chevron, no switches, so the screen implies no default and doesn't answer the end question.
- Props stay whole inside x 92–928, y 302–1428; walls, nightstand and wood bleed full width, dark.
- SFX: dry hits only, in the voice gaps, gated against Whisper's heard words on the finished mix: lamp click (1.84 s), dip thump (14.18: late in the 0.9 s gap after "for good.", mostly its 1.5–3 kHz paper slap so it reads on a phone speaker, 4.2 dB under the voice), case flap (17.04), peg clunk (22.98), top slap (27.94, clear of the phantom word Whisper sometimes hears in the hold). Speech-to-SFX ratio 50 dB. The pillow whump had no clear gap and stays silent (the pillow still squashes).

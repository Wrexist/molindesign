# Treatment · Your phone isn't dead, it's cold

Factory #27 (`coldbattery`), director's plan before the scenes. Script and sources: `BRIEF.md`, `vo/script.json`. Engine, gates and sound: `../deletephoto/` (latest passing film); the look is its own.

## Two benchmarks
- **Learn from `forceclose` (#25, 7.5 first round) and `deletephoto` (#26):** the viewer's own phone on frame 0, the maker's page as the proof, one hero stamp inked in a written silence with the thump, every scene built in full under the wipe, the end card is frame 0 at full opacity. **Don't take:** deletephoto's calendar tear (this film must not look like a sequel), and its caption-only hook (frame 0 here has to state the claim with two words and a picture that can be read with the sound off).
- **Learn from `battery` (#3, 455 views, 6/6):** a threat about the phone in your hand plus a check the viewer can do now (their weather app). **Don't take:** a bare % that reads as charge level (battery r1). Here the 50 % is Battery University's *capacity* figure for a typical cell at 0°F, drawn on two paper cells, never on the phone's status bar.

## Three structures
1. **One phone through one cold morning.** Frame 0 is the phone, frosted and dark: DEAD? or COLD?. The viewer checks their weather (18°F, under the 32°F line); Apple's own range (32°–95°F air) is drawn on a paper thermometer; cut-away to the battery (the reaction slows, a typical cell gives about half its capacity at 0°F); back to the phone: the voltage needle dips and it shuts off; 20 minutes later, inside, the frost peels off and it comes back (Apple's sentence); inside pocket; wait before you charge; frame 0 again. One object (the phone) and one gauge (the thermometer) carry the whole film.
2. **A thermometer as the whole film** (a giant strip, a mark per beat). Strong native move, but the phone (the thing the viewer owns) would be small, and the shutdown and the comeback would be words, not pictures.
3. **Cold open on the comeback** (screen relights after 20 minutes). The DM-able beat first, but it answers the frame-0 question before it is asked (strawberry r1: never the answer on frame 0).

**Chosen: 1.**

## The native move
**The frost line.** A paper thermometer rides the left rail from the second line on; it is the film's clock. When its line falls, cut-paper frost (pale ice-blue shards) creeps in from the phone's edges; when it rises, the frost peels back in strips. Every scene change is the same move: the outgoing scene frosts over at its edges and peels away in five vertical strips, left to right, with the next scene already built under it.

## Rules for this film
- Coral only on the hero stamp IT COMES BACK and the YES / NO chips. The 32°F line, OFF and every chip are cream or ice.
- The 50 % is drawn on two paper battery cells as *capacity* (ABOUT 50% OF ITS CAPACITY · TYPICAL BATTERY · vs 80°F · BATTERY UNIVERSITY), never as the phone's battery %. The phone never shows a number for its charge and never shows 0 %; its battery glyph shows the same partial fill before the shutdown and after it comes back.
- Apple's range is *air* temperature: DESIGNED FOR 32°–95°F AIR. Quotes are printed word for word, with "…" at every cut, and a source line under each.
- "Inside pocket" is our tip (no quote marks, no credit that implies a quote). Nothing says "permanent damage". Charging: Apple's own "Charging On Hold" message, re-created and tagged.
- Generic phone drawn by hand (round punch-hole, no notch, no logo); the weather card, Apple's page and the lock-screen chip are simplified re-creations tagged RE-CREATED from their first frame.
- Lower band y 1000–1430 filled every second (the phone, the thermometer bulb, the cells, the coat).

- The plug no longer clicks in: Apple's "Charging On Hold" banner (two lines, verbatim) stays up for the whole beat, the thermometer rises into the band beside it, and WARM FIRST carries the advice. Readcheck needed the banner ≥ 2 s and the line is 2 s long.

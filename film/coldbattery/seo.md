Why does your phone die in the cold? Usually it isn't dead. It's cold. Cold slows the chemistry inside the battery, the voltage drops when the phone asks for power, and the phone shuts itself off to protect its parts. Warm it up and the battery life comes back.

Check your weather app. Apple Support, "If your iPhone or iPad gets too hot or too cold" (published May 14, 2026): "iPhone and iPad devices are designed for use where the ambient temperature is between 0º and 35º C (32º to 95º F)." Samsung gives the same range for Galaxy phones: "The optimal usage temperature for the Galaxy is 32° - 95°F (0° - 35°C)." Google Pixel Help: "If your phone's temperature is below 0°C / 32°F, it may shut down suddenly."

Why it slows down. Battery University, "BU-502: Discharging at High and Low Temperatures": "Cold temperature increases the internal resistance and lowers the capacity. A battery that provides 100 percent capacity at 27°C (80°F) will typically deliver only 50 percent at –18°C (0°F)." That's a typical lithium-ion cell's capacity, not the percent on your status bar, and it varies with battery chemistry.

Why it shuts off. Apple Support, "iPhone battery and performance" (published June 1, 2026): "A battery's impedance will temporarily increase at a low state of charge and in a cold temperature environment." And: "When a device pulls power from a battery with a higher level of impedance, the battery's voltage will drop to a greater degree." Samsung: "An extremely cold environment may cause a greater drop in voltage. In this case, the device might shut down temporarily to preserve its components."

It comes back. Apple: "Battery life will return to normal when you bring your device back to higher ambient temperatures." Battery University: "The reduced capacity at low temperature only applies while the cell is in that condition and will recover in room temperature."

What to do. Keep it in an inside pocket, close to you (our tip, not a maker's words; Battery University notes that "Warming a dying battery in a mobile phone or flashlight in our jeans might provide additional runtime due to improved electrochemical reaction."). And let it warm up before you charge it. Apple: "If your iOS or iPadOS device gets too cold, it might not charge or could stop charging." Samsung: "If you expose your device to temperature extremes, let the device adjust to the optimal usage temperature (32° - 95°F) before use." Battery University, "BU-410: Charging at High and Low Temperatures": "Extreme cold and high heat reduce charge acceptance and the battery should be brought to a moderate temperature before charging."

So, has yours died in the cold? Yes or no.

Fine print: "isn't dead" means a phone that still had charge left when it shut off; a battery at 1% is also simply empty. Apple says shutdowns are more likely at a low charge or with an older battery. The "about half" figure is Battery University's typical number for batteries in general, not a measurement of any phone model. The inside pocket is our advice. This video makes no claim about lasting damage from cold and gives no set time for a phone to come back; that depends on the phone and the room.

## 1. Topic and one hard idea

**A phone that shuts off in the cold usually isn't dead: cold slows the battery's chemistry and raises its resistance, so when the phone asks for power the voltage drops and it shuts itself off to protect its parts. Warm it up and battery life comes back.**

**Backlog hook replaced.** "Your phone hates the cold." is true but breaks no belief and gives the viewer nothing to check. "Your phone isn't dead. It's cold." turns over what the viewer thinks at the bus stop, and the film pays it off with Apple's own sentence ("Battery life will return to normal…").

**"Permanent damage" is out of the voice.** Apple says cold effects are temporary and saves "permanently" for heat. Battery University says charging a Li-ion cell below freezing causes lithium plating and "permanent degradation", but phones limit or block charging when cold (Apple "might not charge", Samsung "charging will be limited"), so "charging a frozen phone ruins it" would overclaim. The voice only says "let it warm up before you charge it", which Samsung and Battery University both say.

**Wording choices tied to sources**
- `hook` "isn't dead": Apple (fact 2) and Samsung (fact 8) say the cold shutdown and battery-life loss are temporary. True for a cold phone with charge left; a phone at 1% is also empty. The picture shows a phone that had charge (e.g. 40% before the cold), never 0%.
- `apple` "designs iPhones for 32 to 95 degrees" = Apple's "designed for use where the ambient temperature is between… 32º to 95º F". On screen: **DESIGNED FOR 32°–95°F AIR · APPLE SUPPORT · MAY 2026** (say AIR: it is ambient, not the phone's own temperature). Samsung's identical range can sit as a small chip: **GALAXY: 32°–95°F · SAMSUNG**.
- `slow` = BU-502 "slowing of the battery's electrochemical reaction". Say chemistry, not "freezes": the battery doesn't freeze.
- `half` is the riskiest number. BU-502 says a battery "will **typically** deliver only 50 percent at –18°C (0°F)" against 27°C (80°F) and that it "differs with battery chemistry". It is not a phone-specific measurement. Voice keeps "a typical battery" and "about"; screen: **0°F: ABOUT 50% vs 80°F · TYPICAL BATTERY · BATTERY UNIVERSITY**. Say what the % is (LESSONS battery r1): it's deliverable capacity, so never draw it as the % on the phone's status bar.
- `off` "Its voltage drops, so it shuts off to protect itself" = Apple fact 5 (impedance rises in cold → voltage drops → shutdown "to preserve these electronic components") and Samsung fact 8 ("shut down temporarily to preserve its components"). Chip: **COLD → HIGHER RESISTANCE → VOLTAGE DROPS · APPLE**. Apple adds that low charge and an aged battery make it more likely; leave that off the voice, maybe a small chip **WORSE ON AN OLD BATTERY** (fact 5, "higher chemical age").
- `back` = Apple's "Battery life will return to normal when you bring your device back to higher ambient temperatures." Hero quote card, word for word, credit and date. Don't promise the % number itself comes back to an exact value.
- `pocket`: no maker says "inside pocket". It is the plain-words version of Apple's "higher ambient temperatures" and BU-502's "warming a dying battery in a mobile phone… in our jeans might provide additional runtime". Keep it as advice, not a quote; credit BU on screen if a credit is needed.
- `charge` = Samsung "let the device adjust… before use", BU-410 "should be brought to a moderate temperature before charging", Apple "might not charge or could stop charging". Never "charging it cold will ruin it" (see §1).

**Kept out on purpose**
- **"Cold permanently damages your battery."** Apple and BU say the cold loss is temporary; only charging below freezing (BU-410, lab-level) is permanent, and phones guard against it.
- **"The battery % lies / it dies at 30%."** Popular claim; Samsung's "even if the screen displays plenty of battery power remaining" is under *battery decline*, not cold. No primary source ties a % number to cold shutdowns.
- **Exact shutdown temperatures for the phone.** Apple gives an air range; Google's 32°F is phone temperature. Neither says "it shuts off at X".
- **Car-dashboard, hair-dryer, rice tips.** Not sourced; heat is the bigger risk (fact 4).
- **EV range in winter.** Mentioned by BU-502 but off-topic.

## 1. Topic and one hard idea

**A battery warms up as it charges, so a warm phone on the charger is normal; when it gets too hot, the phone slows or pauses its own charging (iPhone may hold it at 80 %) to protect the battery, and you can see it in Settings.**

**Backlog hook rewritten.** "A warm phone while charging is normal. Hot is not." is 10 words over two sentences, and "Hot is not" claims hot = damage, which the sources don't say for charging (they say the phone slows, stops, or turns off to protect itself). Kept its first half as **"Warm while charging? That's normal."** (5 words): a belief turned over, sourced word for word in spirit (Apple "These conditions are normal", Samsung "may feel warm under certain conditions, like charging"), and it loops: "…Is yours warm right now? Yes or no." → "Warm while charging? That's normal."

## 2. Hook options

1. **"Warm while charging? That's normal."** (chosen, 5 words) The belief turned over, the viewer's own phone, a question the viewer answers by touch, and a seamless loop from the end line.
2. "Stuck at 80%? Your phone did that." (6, rejected for line 1) The strongest myth, but it's the payoff; frame 0 would give the answer away (LESSONS: frame 0 shows the conflict, never the answer). Used as the twist instead.
3. "Your phone slows charging on purpose." (6, rejected) True (Apple 105105: "gradually reduces the charging current as the battery approaches full charge"), but abstract on frame 0 and also spends the twist.

## 3b. Truth rules for the jury (check every frame)

**Scope**
1. **The 80 % number is iPhone's.** Every frame that shows 80 carries **iPHONE · IF THE BATTERY GETS TOO WARM** (≥ 36 px) in the same frame. Pixel and Samsung pages say "reduce charging speeds" / "charging may pause", with no number: never put 80 on a Pixel or Galaxy drawing.
2. **The 80 % stall has other causes too** (Optimized Battery Charging, Charge Limit; fact 3's own page). The film never says heat is the *only* reason; the voice says "can stall", and the twist ("your phone did that") is true for all three. No chip may say "80% = TOO HOT".
3. **Temperatures are ambient, not phone temperatures.** If the 32–95 °F range appears (optional chip, `stall` or `cool`), it reads **AIR TEMPERATURE · 32–95°F · APPLE · SAMSUNG**. No thermometer prop may print a number on the phone itself; Google's diagnostics give an *estimated* device temperature and we don't show a value.
4. **Pixel diagnostics: Pixel 6a and later**, the stricter of Google's two pages.
5. **"Warm is normal" is never "hot is safe".** The `hook` chip says WARM; the heat strip on the phone must read *warm* (amber) under the hook, and only turn the hot colour at `stall`.

**Hedges kept (copy onto every chip, LESSONS darkmode, 5gbattery)**
- Apple "might limit charging above 80 percent" → voice "can stall", chip **MIGHT LIMIT CHARGING ABOVE 80%**.
- Apple "slows or stops" → never "always stops".
- Samsung "charging may pause", Pixel "may … Reduce charging speeds" → keep "MAY".
- Apple's 105105 sentence continues ("…which can reduce its lifespan"): the quote chip ends in "…", and the film does **not** say charging heat damages the battery (that's overnightcharge's ground and needs the 95 °F condition).

**Re-created screens (tag each RE-CREATED, ≥ 36 px, LESSONS strawberry, notprivate)**
- iPhone lock screen with a charging banner (`stall`): generic phone, our own wording **"Charging On Hold…"** only if the banner quotes Apple's message exactly with "…"; otherwise a plain battery bar at 80 with no banner. Prefer the plain bar (coldbattery already used the banner).
- iPhone **Settings › Battery** (`iphone`): a generic list screen with the header **Battery** and a row that reads only **CHARGING ON HOLD?** as our label (Apple doesn't publish the exact on-screen wording; never invent a log entry, a time or a temperature).
- Pixel **Battery diagnostics** (`pixel`): four state chips in Google's order; **no state highlighted** (it would answer the end question).
- No real UI, no logos, generic phones (LESSONS battery).

**Kept out on purpose**
- No "fast charging damages your battery", no wattages, no charger-brand advice: no primary source in this set says fast charging harms the battery.
- No fire, swelling or explosion imagery: Samsung's swollen-battery line is about a fault, not normal charging heat, and it would turn a calm film into a scare.
- No "take it out of the case" or "don't charge under a pillow": overnightcharge's facts.
- No device temperature numbers (°F on the phone): none of the pages give a "hot" threshold for the phone itself.

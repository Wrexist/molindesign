## 1. Topic and one hard idea

**Apple's own iPhone User Guide says that on a supported iPhone, with the Find My network switch on, you can locate it for up to 24 hours after it's turned off (up to 5 hours in power reserve mode), because over a billion Apple devices detect it by Bluetooth and report its approximate location back to the owner; without that switch, none of this happens, so check yours.**

**Backlog hook rewritten.** "Your iPhone can be found when off." is 7 words (limit 6). Chosen: **"Turned off? Your iPhone's still findable."** (6). The question half is the picture on frame 0 (power-off screen); the answer half breaks the belief. It is unscoped for 2 s, like rice's hook, so the scope lands fast: on screen at about 1.2 s (**SUPPORTED iPHONE · FIND MY NETWORK ON · UP TO 24 H**, held to 5 s) and in the voice at `twist` ("a supported iPhone") and `hours` ("up to 24 hours").

**Scope in the voice, not only in small print (LESSONS restartphone, rice):** "a supported iPhone" (`twist`), "up to 24 hours" (`hours`), "needs a switch called Find My network" (`dip`), "both switches" (`toggle`, because Apple's steps need Find My iPhone on first).

**Own phone only.** The story is "say you lose it"; Apple's page is about locating a lost iPhone. No thief, no tracking another person, no recovery promise. The backlog's thief twist was dropped: Apple's pages never mention theft, and a thief beat drifts toward "you'll get it back", which Apple doesn't say.

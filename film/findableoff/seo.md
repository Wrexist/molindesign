Turned off? Your iPhone's still findable, for a while, if one switch is on. Apple's iPhone User Guide says: "On a supported iPhone, turning on the Find My network allows you to locate your iPhone for up to 24 hours after it's turned off, or up to 5 hours when it's in power reserve mode."

How? Apple says the Find My network is "a crowdsourced network of over a billion Apple devices that use Bluetooth technology to detect the location of an accessory or device, and report their approximate location back to the owner. It's all anonymous and encrypted to protect everyone's privacy." So a switched-off iPhone with no Wi-Fi and no cellular can still be spotted by Bluetooth, and its approximate location goes back to the owner.

The catch is one switch. Apple: "To make sure that you can find your devices even if they're powered off or disconnected, check that you turned on Find My network."

Check yours (about 10 seconds): Settings › [your name] › Find My › Find My iPhone. Turn on Find My iPhone and Find My network. Apple's steps: "Tap Find My iPhone, then turn on Find My iPhone." And first: "Before you can use the Find My app to locate a lost iPhone, you need to connect the device to your Apple Account."

Same screen, separate switch: "Send Last Location: If your device's battery charge level becomes critically low, its location is sent to Apple automatically."

What Apple doesn't say: the guide says "supported iPhone" and lists no models on that page, so this video names none. The location is approximate; Apple gives no distance. Every number is "up to". And "Find My availability and features might vary by country or region."

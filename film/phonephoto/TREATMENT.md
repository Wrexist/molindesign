# Treatment · phonephoto ("Why your phone photos look better than real life")

## Benchmarks
- **Learn from qrcode (8.0):** one real, checkable mechanism shown on the prop itself (the torn code really decoded). Here the averaging is real: nine sheets with independent seeded grain, the stack is their per-pixel mean.
- **Learn from hallucinate (8.2):** a real document-like object on frame 0 and an early source chip.
- **Don't take from password / gpus (7.2 / 6.9):** six coral stamps, icon-kit props, labels that disagree with the picture for a beat, a quote placed on the wrong object, long silent holds. One coral stamp; every label keyed to its word; quotes only on what they describe.

## Three structures
1. **Mechanism ladder (chosen):** number → question → "before you tap" → shot dark → sky kept → grainy → stack (hero) → why → night (escalation) → Google's words → frame-0 print revealed as the stack → yes/no. One prop (the tracing-paper sheets) carries the whole film.
2. Before/after reveal: open on a bright night photo, rewind it into fifteen dark frames. Rejected: frame 0 would show the answer.
3. "What if your phone took one photo?": counterfactual of a single dark frame. Rejected: two ideas (hypothetical and mechanism) in 35 s.

## Native move
Tracing-paper sheets. A photo is one sheet; the phone's picture is nine sheets snapped into register; the grain on each is different, so the stack's average is clean. Computed per pixel at init (mean of the sheets, then the same tone lift), never a crossfade to a separate clean image.

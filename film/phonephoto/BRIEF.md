## 1. Topic and one hard idea

**When you tap the shutter, your phone (Pixel and iPhone both say so) blends a burst of deliberately dark, grainy frames into one picture: the grain cancels, the bright sky survives, and at night Google says the result is "brighter than reality". Your photo isn't one moment; it's a blend.**

Story order: the number (one tap, nine photos) → the question (why do phone photos look better than real life?) → the surprise (it was shooting before you tapped) → the trick (every frame too dark on purpose, so the sky doesn't blow out) → the cost (dark = grainy) → **the hero: stack them, the grain cancels** → why it works (grain changes, the scene doesn't) → escalation (night: up to 15) → Google's own words (brighter than reality) → the answer (it's a blend) → a yes/no question that loops into "One tap. Nine photos."

**Native move for this film: the tracing-paper stack.** A photo is shown as a sheet of translucent paper; more light or more frames means more sheets laid on top. Merging = sheets snapping into register. It reads hand-made and it's literally how averaging works.

**Main search phrase: `phone photos look better than real life`** (spoken inside `ask`, "phone photos" landing about 2.6 s, the whole phrase by about 4 s). Show **WHY DO PHONE PHOTOS LOOK BETTER THAN REAL LIFE?** in the upper third from ≈ 2.0 s and in the caption's first line. Secondary phrases for the SEO agent (volumes not checked): `computational photography`, `how does night mode work`, `iphone deep fusion`, `pixel night sight`, `why is my phone camera so good`, `HDR photo explained`.

**DM-able moment:** "Your phone starts shooting the moment you open the camera, before you tap" (Google's own words on screen). Second: the 1 FRAME vs 15 STACKED night pair with "brighter than reality".

**Proposed YouTube title (4–8 words, Title Case, one emoji):** **"Why Phone Photos Look Better Than Real Life 🤔"**. Alternatives: "Your Phone Took Nine Photos, Not One 😮", "How Night Mode Sees in the Dark 🤔".

## 2. Hook options

1. **"One tap. Nine photos."** (chosen; 4 spoken words; on screen `1 TAP · 9 PHOTOS`) A number contradiction the viewer can picture and test against their own habit. Short enough to land by about 1.4 s. 2. "Your phone didn't take one photo. It took nine." (the backlog hook, rejected as written) 9 words, over the 6-word cap; and "your phone" flat-out claims one number for every phone, which no source supports (Google itself says 2–15 depending on conditions). 3. "Your phone was shooting before you tapped." (7 words, rejected as the opener, kept as line 3) The most DM-able fact, but on frame 0 it's a vague claim without a number to anchor it, and it needs the source chip to be believed. It works better as the second surprise after the number.

**Wording choices tied to sources**
- `hook` "One tap. Nine photos.": Apple's "nine images" (fact 1), Google's "9 or 15" (fact 2). Condition chip by 1.5 s names and dates both.
- `ask` is the search phrase as a question, no claim.
- `before` "It starts shooting before you even tap.": Google (opening the app starts capture) and Apple (already shot before the press), facts 1–2. Not true of Night Sight, which starts after the press; the film only says this about the everyday tap.
- `dark` "They're shot too dark, on purpose.": "deliberately underexposed" (fact 3). Avoid "all/every": since 2020 HDR+ with Bracketing adds longer frames (fact 3), and Apple's Deep Fusion adds one long exposure (fact 1).
- `sky` "keeps the bright sky from turning white": "the sky is clipped (white)" when exposed for shadows (fact 4).
- `grain` "dark frames come out grainy": "noise in the shadows" (fact 3); "grain" = noise in plain words.
- `stack` "The grain mostly cancels out": "averaging frames to reduce imaging noise" (fact 6). "Mostly" because it reduces, not removes.
- `why` "Grain changes every frame. The scene doesn't.": our plain-words reason for fact 6 (random noise vs aligned scene). Not a quote.
- `night` "a Pixel stacks up to fifteen": "up to 15 if it is handheld" (fact 7). Pixel, not "phones".
- `reality` "In Google's own words, brighter than reality.": fact 8, the three words exactly.
- `blend` "your photo isn't one moment. It's a blend.": frames span from a fraction of a second up to 5–6 s at night (fact 7); Apple calls it "the fused combination" (fact 1).
- `end` asks the viewer; no claim.

**Kept out on purpose**
- Any claim about "all phones" or Samsung/others (no primary source read; the angle says don't).
- Current per-model frame counts for 2025–26 phones (not published by either company that I found); every count on screen carries its year.
- "AI makes your photos fake" / "AI-generated" framing: Google's merge is described as signal processing; Apple's as machine learning. Don't call the photo fake; the end question leaves it to the viewer.
- Face retouching, beauty filters, Super Res Zoom, Photographic Styles, Samsung moon controversy: each a second idea.
- The √N noise maths and shot vs read noise (true, in Google 2021, but a second idea).

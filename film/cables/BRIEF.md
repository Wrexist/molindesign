# Brief · What if every undersea internet cable was cut?

Long-form episode 1 of Tech, explained on YouTube (16:9, about 5 minutes). It's a stickman "what if" told in second person, with an escalating clock (second, minute, hour, day, week, month). The real events are told as real, and the scenario parts are worded as a scenario.

## Facts on screen and in the voice, with sources

| Claim | Source |
|---|---|
| More than 600 cables, active and planned (570 in service in 2025) | TeleGeography, Submarine Cable FAQ (2026) and "How many submarine cables are there, anyway?" |
| Over 1.5 million km in service (said as "over 900,000 miles", which is about 37 times around Earth's 40,075 km equator) | TeleGeography FAQ (2026) |

## Scenario math (said as rough math)

About 570 in-service cables ÷ about 25 dedicated ships ≈ 23 cables per ship. At two weeks a repair, 23 × 2 = 46 weeks, which is almost a year. It assumes one break per cable and ships working nonstop, and the frame says so.

## Scenario beats (worded as "what if", not fact)

- Second 1: messages abroad don't arrive and routers find no other path off the continent. Land links to Canada and Mexico stay up, which is why the script says "off the continent".
- Minute 1: local texts, local sites and shows from local caches keep working. Sites hosted only overseas and calls abroad fail.
- Hour 1: a tourist's card fails when the issuing bank is abroad, traders can't see London, and companies using an overseas cloud region go dark. Satellites can't take the load (0.37%).

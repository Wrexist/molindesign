## 1. Topic and one hard idea

**In Spotify's own words, every skip, play, search and save shapes your "taste profile"; in Spotify Research's 2025 system each song becomes 80 numbers, a point in a big space where songs land close when people put them in the same playlists (and when they sound alike), and each listener is a point too, which a newly streamed artist can move within minutes. So a run of your kid's songs pulls your picks, and Spotify gives you a switch to exclude them.**

Story order: the viewer's own "why this song?" (frame-0 test) → every skip feeds the taste profile (stake) → the search question → **80 numbers per song (2025 research)** → flattened to a map (our drawing) → **hero: same playlists land close** → sound alike too (the honest second encoder) → you are a point too → listeners near you (Stål's "fifth artist") → **dip: your kid's songs move you in minutes** → exclude from taste profile → yes/no → frame 0.

**Native move: the string pull.** Each made-up playlist is a cream string; when it tightens, its pins slide together on the map. On `sound` a second kind of tie appears (a paper waveform tag clipped between two pins) so "sounds alike" is visibly a different signal, not another playlist.

**Main search phrase: `how does spotify know what you like`** (spoken word for word in `ask`, ≈ 2.7–5.0 s Ming). Show **HOW DOES SPOTIFY KNOW WHAT YOU LIKE?** in the upper third from the start of `ask` and in the caption's first line.

**DM-able moment:** "Songs in the same playlists land close." (send it to the friend whose playlists are why your Discover Weekly is weird). Second: "Your kid's songs move you in minutes" (send it to every parent whose recommendations went all kids' songs).

## 6. Risks and doubts

- **The myth line is out.** The backlog wanted "it doesn't listen to the music" only if sourced; Spotify Research says the opposite (audio encoder). The film says the true version ("So do songs that sound alike"). Packaging loses its hard myth point; it is a threat-and-fix film instead.
- **One research system, not "the app".** The 80 numbers, the audio and playlist encoders and the "within minutes" refresh are from one 2025 Spotify Research framework that the post says is "deployed in production" and used for Home shelves, search and Home ranking. The voice scopes "Its research"; the screen dates it. Don't say Discover Weekly works this way.
- **"Kid's songs" is our example.** The source's example is "streaming a new artist". Spotify itself names "kids and family music" as excludable, which supports the example, but the jury may want the source's own example on screen: the time strip carries it.
- **Stål 2021 is five years old** and an illustration ("Imagine…"). It's credited as 2021 on screen and used only for the "listeners near you" idea, which the 2026 page's "general tastes and behaviors of users" also supports.
- **Exclude availability** "depends on your country and subscription plan" (Spotify's words); it's in US Spotify as far as the page shows, but not checked in an app (no app UI is drawn anyway).
- **Packaging** scored 5/6 honestly (6/6 by the letter); the hook is a stake, not a broken belief.
- **No AI tag rule:** no line, chip, caption or description may say the video or voice is AI-made.

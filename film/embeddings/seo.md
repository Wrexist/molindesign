How does Spotify know what you like? Spotify's own page says skipping, listening, searching and saving all shape your "taste profile". In Spotify's research, each song becomes 80 numbers, a point in a big space, and you're a point too. Your kids' songs can move yours within minutes, and there's a setting to exclude them.

Your taste profile. Spotify, "Understanding recommendations on Spotify" (last updated Oct 23, 2026): "As you engage with Spotify, actions such as searching, listening, skipping, or saving to Your Library influence our interpretation of your taste. We call this your “taste profile,” and it gives our algorithms an indication of what you're interested in and how you like to listen."

80 numbers per song. Spotify Research's "Generalized user representations for large-scale recommendations" (Sep 18, 2025; paper at RecSys 2025, Industry) describes one system that Spotify says is "Deployed in production". It learns song embeddings two ways. "Collaborative encoder: learns track embeddings from playlist co-occurrence…" and "Audio encoder: learns track embeddings directly from audio features, capturing acoustic similarity." Then: "These 80-dimensional track embeddings are fused with contextual, demographic, and onboarding signals…" So songs in the same playlists land close, and so do songs that sound alike.

You're a point too. Same post: "Each listener is mapped to a high-dimensional embedding within a stable vector space…" The post doesn't say how many numbers that is. The 80 is per song, never per listener.

Listeners near you. Oskar Stål, Spotify's VP of Personalization, on Spotify's newsroom (Oct 13, 2021): "Imagine you and another person have similar music tastes. You have four of the same top artists, but your fifth artists are different. We would take those two near-matches and think, ‘Hmm, maybe each person would like the other’s fifth artist’ and suggest it."

Within minutes. Spotify Research (Sep 18, 2025): "NRT inference, triggered by user activity events (e.g., streaming a new artist), refreshes embeddings within minutes." Kids' songs are our example; Spotify's example is a new artist.

The fix. Spotify's page: "Exclude from taste profile: When you exclude content from your taste profile, it will have less influence on your future recommendations. You can exclude tracks, playlists or kids and family music." Spotify lists it among controls whose "availability depends on your country and subscription plan".

Has one song hijacked your mix? Yes or no.

Fine print: the 80 numbers per song and the "within minutes" refresh come from one Spotify Research system (Sep 18, 2025), not from the app's exact recipe. Songs and listeners sit on separate sheets because the post doesn't say they share one space. Excluding gives content "less influence"; Spotify doesn't say it removes it. The Exclude setting's availability depends on your country and subscription plan, in Spotify's words.

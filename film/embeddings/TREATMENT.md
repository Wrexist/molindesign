# Treatment · How Spotify knows your taste

Factory #24 (`embeddings`), director's plan before the scenes. Script and sources: `BRIEF.md`, `vo/script.json`.

## Two benchmarks
- **Learn from `whatif-password` (#23):** one hand-made hero prop that stays on screen, count/label and state on the same frame, a hero stamp in a written silence, frame 0 gliding ≥ 300 px. **Don't take:** a 37 s run (this one aims at ~30 s) and a 10.4 MB upload (this one is encoded to land near 9 MB).
- **Learn from `energy` (#22):** the end card is frame 0 at full opacity with YES / NO. **Don't take:** its empty lower third (2.5–9.5 s, 21–23 s). Here every scene is laid out from y 1430 up: the card, the number tape, the map's cork rail and a quote shelf that always carries one strip.

## Three structures
1. **One map, built up** (song → 80 numbers → a dot on a paper map → strings pull playlists together → a LISTENERS sheet over it → YOU gets yanked → the fix). One mechanism the whole way, one hero prop that stays from 8 s to the end line.
2. **Two maps side by side** (songs left, listeners right). Honest about "two spaces", but each map is 390 px wide: too small to read at 540 px.
3. **Cold open on the yanked YOU pin**, then rewind. Strong dip, but frame 0 would show the answer, not the viewer's own test.

**Chosen: 1.** The listeners never share the song map: they sit on a see-through acetate sheet tabbed LISTENERS that slides over it.

## The native move
**The string pull.** A cream string threads three paper record pins (ROAD TRIP · EXAMPLE) and tightens; the pins slide together. A paper waveform clip does the same for two pins that sound alike (a different tie, so it reads as a different signal). The dip reuses it: a string from the kids' pins yanks YOU across the LISTENERS sheet.

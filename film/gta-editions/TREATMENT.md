# Two prices · treatment (Delta #11)

## Benchmarks
- **film/gta-pc/** (Delta #8, held at 7.0–7.2): learn its engine (voice-first `at(id, word)` keying, the VAD gate in `vo/vad.js`, the slanted pink/teal cut with the next scene drawn under it, the dissolve home into frame 0, `fontStretch` re-applied after every font change). Don't take its look: a monitor, a wait chart and chips read as an infographic dashboard, and its first cut froze 1.5–3 s near the 5 s drop-off.
- **film/qrcode/** (Tech, explained, passed at 8.0): learn that a physical prop the viewer can read and that changes state (a torn code) carries a film better than any diagram. Don't take its single-scene length: here the prop has to keep changing every ≤ 1.2 s.

## Three structures
1. **Comparison table**: two columns, rows tick in (price, bonus, items, shops, box). Clear, but it is exactly the dashboard look juries called a template. Rejected.
2. **Unboxing**: open both editions' boxes. Rejected: Ultimate has no box (fact 9), so the structure itself would lie.
3. **The tag and its stub** (chosen): one physical object, two cardboard price tags in a shop window. The extra piece of the Ultimate tag *is* the $20: it tears off, gets stamped +$20, feeds a receipt printer that prints what it buys, then comes back for the vote. Every scene re-uses the window, the counter and the stub, so the film feels like one place, not slides.

## The native move
The perforated stub tearing off the Ultimate tag in the written pause after "gap." (rip), dropping onto the counter and taking the +$20 stamp (stamp). The poster is that frame.

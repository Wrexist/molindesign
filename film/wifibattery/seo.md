Does turning off Wi-Fi save battery? Barely. Apple says Wi-Fi and Bluetooth "are designed to draw minimal power from the battery when they aren't connected to a network or accessory", and its advice is to keep both on.
And on iPhone, the Wi-Fi button in Control Center doesn't even turn Wi-Fi off: it disconnects you, Wi-Fi stays available for AirDrop and Location Services, and auto-join comes back by 5 a.m. To really turn it off, go to Settings › Wi-Fi. The one exception: Google says to turn Bluetooth off to stretch a dying battery. So check Settings › Battery for what really drains yours, not the toggles.

Apple, "Charge and maintain your iPhone battery", section "How Wi-Fi and Bluetooth affect your battery": "Wi-Fi and Bluetooth are designed to draw minimal power from the battery when they aren't connected to a network or accessory. For the best experience on your iPhone, keep Wi-Fi and Bluetooth turned on." Apple's reason to keep them on is "the best experience".

Apple, "Use Bluetooth and Wi-Fi in Control Center": "When you toggle the Wi-Fi or Bluetooth buttons in Control Center, your device immediately disconnects from Wi-Fi and Bluetooth accessories." "Both Wi-Fi and Bluetooth will continue to be available", for AirDrop, AirPlay, Apple Watch, Location Services and more. "While you disable Wi-Fi, auto-join for any nearby Wi-Fi networks is also disabled until:" you turn Wi-Fi on in Control Center, connect to a network in Settings › Wi-Fi, "walk or drive to a new location", it's "5 a.m. local time", or "restart your device". To turn them fully off: "Open the Settings app, tap Wi-Fi, and turn off Wi-Fi. Open the Settings app, tap Bluetooth, and turn off Bluetooth." Scope: this is about the iPhone and iPad Control Center buttons only; the video makes no claim about Android's Quick Settings tile.

Apple, "If the battery in your iPhone or iPad drains too quickly": "Wi-Fi uses less battery power than cellular networks." "Both Wi-Fi and cellular connections use less energy when used in places with high signal strength. If you know you'll be in an area with no coverage for some time, enable Airplane Mode."

Apple, "Check battery usage on your iPhone": "Open Settings. Tap Battery." "Tap View All Battery Usage to view the battery usage percentage over the last eight days."

Google, Pixel Phone Help, "Stretch a low battery's power on Pixel phone", and Android Help, "Get the most life from your Android device's battery", under "Limit connectivity & location": "Turn off Bluetooth." and "Use Wi-Fi instead of mobile data." Both are tips to stretch a low battery. Google notes "Some of these steps may vary based on your device." On a Pixel, battery use by app is in Settings › Battery › Battery usage.

Fine print: "Minimal" is not zero, and neither Apple nor Google gives a number; turning a radio off can still help a little, more so on a dying battery or with no coverage.

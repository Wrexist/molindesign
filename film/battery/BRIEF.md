## 1. Topic and one hard idea

**Your phone's battery is losing capacity right now, and the thing that ages it fastest isn't use: it's sitting full (and hot).** That's why Apple, Google and Samsung all now let your phone stop at 80%.

**The film is a viewer-as-subject puzzle** (the fyp lesson: making the viewer the subject gave the best opening yet). Line 1 is true for every phone in every hand: lithium-ion capacity fades from day one (fact 2). Line 2 hands them the check: Battery Health in Settings, a number below 100% on most phones that aren't brand new. The twist is that a full battery ages faster than a half-full one, with one concrete pair of numbers (80% vs 96% after a year, fact 6). The end asks "what's your battery health?", a one-number comment that loops back to "Your phone battery is already shrinking."

**Main search phrase: `battery health`.** Spoken at about 3.5 s in line `check` and again in `end`. Show it on screen in the first 2 s and in the caption's first line. Secondary phrases for the SEO agent: `why is my battery health going down`, `iPhone battery health`, `charge to 80 percent`, `iPhone 80% limit`, `should I charge my phone to 100`, `phone battery wear out`. I didn't check search volumes.

**DM-able moment:** lines `year` → `forty`: "Leave a battery full for a year at room temp. It keeps about 80% of its capacity. Leave it at 40% instead? About 96%." It's a pair of numbers people send to the friend who charges to 100% every night.

**Proposed YouTube title (4–8 words, Title Case, one emoji):** **"Why Your Phone Battery Is Shrinking 🤔"**. Alternatives: "Why Your Phone Stops Charging at 80% 🤔" and "What Charging to 100% Does to Your Phone 😮".

## 2. Hook options

1. **"Your phone battery is already shrinking."** (chosen, 6 words) Second person, about the viewer's own phone, a contradiction ("my battery is fine"), and literally true: Apple says capacity diminishes as lithium-ion "chemically ages" (fact 2), and calendar aging runs even in storage (facts 6–7). "Shrinking" means capacity, which the screen makes explicit (a battery icon whose outline physically contracts). It sets up the self-check in line 2 and the closing question.
2. "Charging your phone to 100% every night is aging it." (the sheet hook, rejected) 10 words, nearly twice the limit, and it gives the answer on frame 0 (strawberry r1 lesson). It's also slightly off: an iPhone or Pixel with Optimized/Adaptive Charging on (the default on iPhone) waits at 80% overnight (facts 9, 11), so for many viewers it isn't sitting at 100% all night.
3. "Why does your phone stop at 80%?" (7 words, backup) A real puzzle for iPhone 15+/Pixel/Samsung owners who saw the limit, but one word over and only works for people who've noticed it. Good YouTube title material.

**Wording choices tied to sources**
- `twist` says "**ages it faster**", not "ruins" or "kills": Samsung says "can reduce its lifespan", Apple "reduce the wear". Isac's no-overclaim note.
- `year`/`eighty`/`forty` say "**a battery**", not "your phone": Table 3 is cells in storage, not a phone in use. The screen labels it **ESTIMATE · Battery University** and **77 °F**. "Room temp" = 25 °C = 77 °F.
- `heat` gives Apple's 95 °F threshold exactly and says "**can**" do permanent damage, as Apple does. Put **APPLE** on the chip.
- `drain` says "wears it too", softer than the source table implies, because a phone's "0%" is not the cell's absolute empty (inferred: phones keep a reserve; I didn't find a first-party page saying so). Don't put a cycle number on screen for this line, or use BU's "~300 vs ~1,000 cycles" with ESTIMATE.
- `limit`: iPhone's 80% is a 15-and-later feature, Pixel's 6a-and-later, Samsung's One UI 6.1+ ("Maximum"). Screen: three generic drawn phones each with an **80%** chip, small type "iPhone 15+ · Pixel 6a+ · Galaxy, One UI 6.1+".
- `night` says your phone "**can** wait at 80", because Apple says "can delay" and Samsung's Adaptive switches modes while you sleep. No safety claim either way.

**Kept out on purpose**
- **"Charging overnight is dangerous / will make it explode", "never let it go below 20%", "battery memory", "don't use the phone while charging", "only use the original charger".** None of these are in the sources. Isac's note excludes danger myths; the film says nothing about safety.
- **"Charging to 80% doubles your battery life."** BU Table 4 maps 4.20 V → 300–500 cycles and 4.06 V (81% energy) → 600–1,000, but a phone's displayed 80% isn't the same as a 4.06 V cutoff, and phones vary. Too shaky for narration.
- **Fast charging wears batteries faster.** Mixed evidence, not in the first-party docs. Out.
- **Samsung's battery health %.** Samsung shows status through the Samsung Members app diagnostics, not a Settings percentage; I didn't verify a current path, so the film shows iPhone and Pixel for the self-check.

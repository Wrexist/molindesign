# Treatment · "Sneeze"

**Promise by 0:05:** "Tomorrow morning, the Sun sneezes. Right at us." The cloud flies straight into the lens.
**Three questions by 0:20:** what breaks first, how long it lasts, should you be worried. They're pinned up as cards, then the first quest lands: how much warning?

| Part (card) | Question asked | Payoff | Quotable line on screen |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 · How much warning? | how long until it hits? | 17 hours, then 15–60 min from the doorbell camera | "less time than a pizza delivery" |
| 2 · It happened before | has this happened? | 1859: Carrington's flash, Caribbean auroras, zapped telegraphs | "Free electricity from space. Sounds great." / "Today, it's everything." |
| 3 · What breaks first? | what goes first? | Starlink losses, GPS tractors, a lake on your maps app | "A minor one." |
| 4 · The lights go out | why is the grid the real danger? | current shoved into long lines; Quebec in 90 s | "And tomorrow's storm is the big one." |
| 5 · How long does it last? | how long are we in the dark? | house-size transformers take years; Lloyd's numbers | (the $2.6 trillion receipt) |
| 6 · Should you worry? | how likely is it? | up to 12% a decade; the 2012 near miss; an hour still helps | "Old school beats no school." |

**Split-screen contrast:** 1859 (one telegraph plugged in) against today (everything plugged in).
**Ending:** the weird upside, auroras in Miami, the perfect selfie you can't post. Then the next video is named out loud: the cables episode.

**Look:** one key light per scene, rim-lit characters on contact shadows, a handheld camera that never stops drifting, glows for anything hot, a Sun with a face. Chapter cards slam in on a hard hit in the 1.6 s pause before each part, then park top-left as a label.
**Voice:** Chatterbox (MIT, free), picked by Isac over Ming and ElevenLabs as the least robotic. One fixed seed and settings for every line, whole-line synthesis, every line levelled to the same loudness, no per-line tempo changes.

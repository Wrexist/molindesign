Sources
• NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center: L1 warning satellites about 1 million miles out, 15 to 60 minutes of warning; the 1859 Carrington Event's ~17.5-hour transit
• Loomis (1860) and NASA, "A Super Solar Flare" (2008): telegraph sparks, the Washington operator struck on the forehead, Boston–Portland lines working with batteries unplugged
• SpaceX and CIRES/NASA (2022): 38 of 49 new Starlink satellites lost to a minor storm
• Fox Weather / KTVU (May 2024): GPS-guided tractors in North Dakota off by about 10 feet
• Hydro-Québec and NASA: 13 March 1989, the grid collapsed in about 90 seconds, about 6 million people without power for about 9 hours
• Lloyd's / AER (2013), "Solar storm risk to the North American electric grid": 20–40 million Americans, up to 1–2 years, $0.6–2.6 trillion
• Riley (2012), Space Weather: about 12% per decade; later estimates as low as about 0.5%
• Baker et al. (2013) and NASA (2014): the July 2012 CME missed Earth by about a week

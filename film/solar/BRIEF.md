# Brief · What if the biggest solar storm in history hit Earth tomorrow?

Long-form episode 2 of Tech, explained on YouTube (16:9, about 4 minutes). It's a stickman "what if" in six parts, each one a spoken question answered before the next (the LONGFORM.md "quests"). Real events are told as real, and tomorrow's storm is worded as a scenario. Jokes are carried by the picture as much as the voice.

## Jokes and how each lands

- The Sun "sneezes": the cloud of plasma is a character with a face.
- The warning satellite is a doorbell camera ("ding dong").
- Less warning than a pizza delivery; the pizza isn't trying to fry the grid.
- The 1859 operator is zapped, gets lightning hair and soot, then gives a thumbs up: "Free electricity from space. Sounds great."
- "A storm that scientists called minor" → gauge on MINOR → "A minor one."
- The farmer: "NOPE."; your maps app: turn left into a lake, with 100% confidence.
- The fridge knows first, then your nose (flies).
- The perfect Miami aurora selfie, and "✕ NO SIGNAL".

## Scenario wording

"Tomorrow" is a scenario: the voice says what happens "tomorrow", and every number comes from a real past event or a published estimate. The "1–2 years" and "$2.6 trillion" are the top of the Lloyd's range and are said as "up to".

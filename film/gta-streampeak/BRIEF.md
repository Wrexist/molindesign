**Truth-proofing (read first).**
- **Whose number.** Every viewer number is a **Streams Charts measurement** (a third-party analytics company), not Rockstar's, Twitch's or YouTube's. Screen carries `STREAMS CHARTS DATA` on the hook and on every number beat.
- **Which 7.9 million.** It is the **peak concurrent viewers** across tracked livestream platforms (Twitch, YouTube, Kick, SOOP, CHZZK), at one moment. Not unique people, not total views, not players, not sales. The voice says "live viewers" and "all at the same time, on streams"; never "people", "players" or "sold". The Nov 18 article calls it **preliminary**; the Nov 19 day-one recap repeats it (7,915,475). Screen: `PEAK · STREAMS CHARTS DATA` (add `PRELIMINARY` if the director has room; the voice rounds to 7.9 so either version stays true).
- **The platform peaks do not add up.** Twitch 3.31M + YouTube 3.22M + Kick 1.50M + others = about 8.16M, more than 7.92M, because "these preliminary platform peaks may have occurred at different times" (Streams Charts FAQ). **Never show them as slices of the 7.9M** (no pie, no stacked bar summing to 7.9). The film only uses Twitch's peak, on its own.
- **The old record is Valorant's *closed beta*** (April 2020, 1,737,267), not Valorant's 1.0 launch. Streams Charts' own table names it "Valorant (Closed Beta)". The voice says "Valorant's closed beta".
- **"More than four and a half times"** is Streams Charts' own wording ("more than 4.5 times"); our math agrees: 7,915,475 / 1,737,267 = 4.556 (derived, below).
- **"The biggest game in Twitch history"** = Streams Charts: GTA VI "has now become the most popular gaming category in Twitch history by Peak Viewers, surpassing League of Legends (3.11 million) and Fortnite (3.04 million)", and its FAQ: "This is a record among games, not across all Twitch categories." Screen scopes it: `#1 GAME EVER ON TWITCH · BY PEAK VIEWERS`. Never "biggest stream ever" or "Twitch record" unscoped.
- **No sales.** Rockstar and Take-Two have published no GTA 6 sales number as of 2026-11-20 (caller's statement; Rockstar Newswire is JS-rendered and I could not read it with curl, so this is **not independently verified** and is kept out of the voice and the screen). The film only says "Watching isn't buying" and shows `VIEWERS ≠ SALES`, which needs no source.
- **Not used:** Kick's ranking (the Nov 18 article says Kick was 2nd behind Minecraft; the Nov 19 recap says "most popular game ever on Twitch and Kick", a conflict), hours watched (125.47M, true but a second hero number), streamer names (IShowSpeed, IlloJuan), Variety's internet-traffic figures, prices.
- **Dates.** No date words in the voice ("today", "yesterday"). Re-read both Streams Charts pages before posting: the hub keeps updating and a later peak could replace 7.91M. If the headline number moves past 7.95M or the 4.5x changes, re-voice the hook.

## 1. One hard idea
**At its launch peak, 7.9 million live viewers were watching GTA 6 livestreams at the same moment, more than four and a half times the previous game-launch record (Valorant's closed beta, 1.74 million, 2020), by Streams Charts' count.** Kicker: on Twitch alone it is now the most-watched game category ever by peak viewers.

Main search phrase: `GTA 6 viewers` ("GTA 6" spoken 0.2-1.0 s, "live viewers" by ~2.7 s). Second: `GTA 6 Twitch record`, `GTA 6 streams`.

DM-able moment: one GTA 6 tower of viewers next to four and a half Valorant towers that together only just reach its height (`×4.5`).

1. **"GTA 6. 7.9 million live viewers."** **Chosen.** 6 spoken words, GTA 6 in the first second (SAFE-EDIT rule 6), the number is the news. "Live viewers" is the honest unit (concurrent, not people or sales).

3. "GTA 6 broke streaming by 4.5x." Rejected: gives the payoff away and "broke streaming" overclaims.

## 1. Topic and one hard idea

**The glitches people look for in AI video (extra fingers, garbled text, wrong shadows) are getting harder to spot, and in a 2024 study people judged real versus fake about as well as chance. So the check that still works takes five seconds and isn't about pixels: who posted it first, the original upload, and an AI label or Content Credentials, remembering that a missing label proves nothing.**

Story order: the test (is this real? five seconds) → the question with the search phrase → what everyone does (hunt for glitches) → the classic tells, shown in our drawn clip → **but** they're fading (the clean re-draw) → **dip:** 2024, people near a coin flip → turn: stop staring at pixels → **hero: check who posted it first** (loupe onto the name, stamp, hold) → how: tap the name, find the original upload → look for an AI label or Content Credentials → **no label proves nothing** → the question that loops into line 1.

**Native move: the loupe that leaves the picture.** For the first half it hunts across the pixels like the viewer does; on `stop` it slides off the clip and down onto the poster's name. The whole argument is that one move, and it reads with the sound off.

**Main search phrase: `how to tell if a video is ai`** (spoken word for word in `ask`, ≈ 2.4–5 s Ming). Show **HOW TO TELL IF A VIDEO IS AI** in the upper third from ≈ 2.4 s and in the caption's first line. Secondary (volumes not checked): `is this video ai`, `ai video detector`, `how to spot ai videos`, `content credentials`, `ai label youtube`, `ai generated label tiktok`.

**DM-able moment:** "In a 2024 study, people guessed right about half the time" (send it to the friend who says they can always tell). Second: "No label? That proves nothing."

**Proposed YouTube title:** **"AI Videos Are Fooling Your Eyes 😱"**. Alternatives: "How To Tell If A Video Is AI 🤔" (pure search, no consequence verb), "Your Eyes Can't Spot AI Videos 😱" (stronger claim than the study allows; avoid).

## 6. Risks

- **Truth: `hunt`'s "most people"** is framing, not a measured number. Juries may flag it; the fallback is "We all hunt for glitches."
- **Truth: image guide used for video.** Kamali is about still images; the film applies it to a video frame. The on-screen tag says "NORTHWESTERN'S 2024 GUIDE", and the voice never says the guide is about video.
- **Truth: the 50 % is an average** across images, audio, video and audiovisual media, with stimuli from 2024 or earlier. The voice says "a 2024 study" and "about half"; the card says the media types.
- **Look: the clip must not look generated.** Cut-paper edges, visible paper grain, no smooth gradients, nothing morphs. The re-draw on `fading` is a paper flip, not a morph.
- **Packaging vs. honesty:** the title "Fooling Your Eyes" is supported by fact 3 ("struggled to distinguish") but don't go stronger ("can't", "impossible").
- **No AI tag rule:** the film is about AI video, but no line or chip may say the video or voice is AI-made.
- **Hero sound gate:** the 1.2 s hero silence and the 0.6 s coin silence are the only gaps that can hold hits; reserve them before the first render (gta-songs r2).

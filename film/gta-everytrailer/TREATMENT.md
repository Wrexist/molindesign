# Treatment: GTA 5 vs GTA 6, every trailer side by side (Delta long-form L1)

16:9, 1920x1080, 30 fps, about 4:43. Ming US_James, procedural SFX only, no music. Facts: `BRIEF.md`.

## Benchmarks
- **Learn from `film/cables`** (our first 16:9 long-form): chapter cards that slam in and then live on as a header, a slow push on every scene, a progress rail. **Don't take** its stickman cast and busy cartoon set: this film's subject is the footage, so the frame must stay clean (Apple/Linear: one ink ground, cream type, one hot accent).
- **Learn from `film/gta-sametrailer` and `film/gta-daysales`**: official clips as evidence with a label on each, GTA 5 cool / GTA 6 warm, sources typed in our font. **Don't take** footage as wallpaper: here a clip is always inside a framed panel with a note under it, and our slate sits under every panel when no clip is on.

## Three structures
1. Trailer by trailer, chronological (2011 → 2026). Clear, but it reads as a list and buries the comparison.
2. Two halves: all of GTA 5, then all of GTA 6. The viewer has to hold four minutes in their head.
3. **Six chapters, each a side-by-side pair (chosen).** Each chapter is one of the "moves" (first trailer, heroes, map, gameplay reveal, last trailer, launch day); every chapter puts GTA 5 left and GTA 6 right on the same frame, and the last chapter closes the playbook clipboard opened in the cold open.

## Native move: the split seam
Two 16:9 panels (870x490) either side of a seam at x 960 with a VS badge. The seam carries the beat: it turns into a MATCH tick when a pair lines up (wild, face, boats, dusk, robbery), it shoves left when "one thing doesn't match" (93 million views), and chapter cards open from it (the seam wipe). A six-pip rail at top right fills one pip per chapter.

# GTA was almost canceled (Delta, pillar 3: GTA history)

Slug `gta-origin` · backlog row 38 · researched 2026-11-11 · channel **Delta** (never Tech, explained). American English, US audience, own voice (Ming US_James, seed 7, block copied from film/gta-preload), own flat hand-built paper graphics, **no music**, no Rockstar footage, logo, key art or box art, no "AI" anywhere. Follows gta-preload (pillar 6), so the pillar rotation holds.

**Truth-proofing (read first).** The backlog hook "GTA started as a bug" is **not** what the people who made it say, so it is not used:
- The game started as **Race'n'Chase**, a cops-and-robbers driving game (design document dated 22 March 1995, posted by Mike Dailly; DMA's Steve Hammond: "Race 'n' Chase was a cops 'n' robbers game until playing the robbers proved to be more fun").
- The bug came **later**, in a project that was crashing and close to cancellation. The only first-hand account of it I could reach is **Gary Penn** (DMA Design, worked on the original GTA), and he hedges: "One day, **I think it was a bug**, the police suddenly became mental and aggressive." So the voice says "One developer thinks it was a bug" and the screen credits him by name. "Testers loved it" is press colour, not Penn's words, and is not used; Penn's words are "That was awesome, so that stayed in."
- The film never says the game "started as a bug", never says the bug alone "saved" it as a fact (see §3 note on fact 6), and never gives an exact 1997 release day.

## 1. One hard idea
**The first GTA was almost canceled, and what turned it around was cops who suddenly tried to ram you off the road, which one of its developers thinks was a bug.** One hero object: **the paper police car**.

Search phrases: `GTA almost canceled` (spoken from 0.2 s, on frame 0), `Race'n'Chase` (spoken ~7 s, title card). The SEO agent captions both, plus "first GTA 1997".

DM-able moment: `ram` + `bug`. The cops going rogue by accident and the team keeping it ("He called it awesome. It stayed in.") is the thing people send the friend who rages at GTA's wanted level.

## 2. Hook options
1. **"GTA was almost canceled."** **Chosen.** 4 words, GTA is the first spoken word (0.2 s) and the first word on screen. A stake anyone gets in 3 s, no insider names. First-hand: Penn, "It was almost canned. The publisher, BMG Interactive, wanted to can it."

3. "GTA started as a bug." (backlog) Rejected: false as worded. It started as Race'n'Chase in 1995; the bug came during development.

**Note on fact 5-6 and the word "saved" (line `why`).** Penn lists the near-cancel, the crashes, the handling, then the police moment as "an awesome moment" that "stayed in". Gamasutra's own summary is "a serendipitous moment in the development of the game turned things around for the team", and PC Gamer's headline is "saved by psycho police car bug". So "So what saved it?" is the press's reading of Penn's account, answered in the voice only with attributed lines ("One developer thinks it was a bug"). The `bug` beat's credit chip carries the source: `GARY PENN · DMA DESIGN · GAMASUTRA 2011`.

**Derived:** none spoken. No "29 years" or "almost 30 years": with the 1997 day disputed, Nov 19, 2026 is either just under or just over 29 years after it, so the film shows two year cards (`1997`, `NOV 19 2026`) and lets the viewer do the gap.

**Not used:**
- "Testers loved it / abandoned the missions to chase police": common in secondary press (LADbible, GamingBolt), not in Penn's words. Out.
- "Players originally played the cop": Wikipedia and wikis say so; the 1995 doc as reported by Engadget has cops and robbers both. Not needed. Out.
- Dundee: DMA was in Dundee, but the move to Edinburgh dates are unclear in what I read; a location adds nothing to the one idea. Out of voice; the director may show `DMA DESIGN · SCOTLAND` only.
- Development length ("four years", "30 months"): sources disagree (Engadget says "two and a half years"). Out.
- Any sales number: none needed. Out.

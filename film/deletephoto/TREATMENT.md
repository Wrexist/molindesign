# Treatment · Deleted photos aren't really gone

Factory #26 (`deletephoto`), director's plan before the scenes. Script and sources: `BRIEF.md`, `vo/script.json`. Template for structure, gates and sound: `../forceclose/` (passed round 1 at 7.5); the look is its own.

## Two benchmarks
- **Learn from `forceclose` (#25, 7.5 first round):** the viewer's own habit on frame 0, the maker's page as proof, one hero stamp inked in a written silence with the thump, every scene built in full under the wipe, the end card is frame 0 at full opacity. **Don't take:** its card-flick wipe and app-card fan (this film must not look like a sequel), and its 1.43 s frozen hold reading as a stall (keep the still to the stamp's own hold).
- **Learn from `incognito` (#21, 1,281 views, 6/6):** the hook is the maker's own sentence turned on the viewer ("it goes to the Recently Deleted album"), the proof is a re-created, tagged screen. **Don't take:** a label sitting over a group it doesn't name, and a "shredded" prop that reads as tidy at 540 px (the shred at the payoff tilts ≥ 15°, gaps ≥ 12 px, pieces fall).
- **And from `embeddings` (#24, parked):** a strip or stamp left beside a new subject reads as a claim about it. Every label, credit and stamp peels before the next subject lands.

## Three structures
1. **The camera roll, the bin and the calendar.** Frame 0 is the viewer's deleted photos (a paper camera roll) behind a padlock; the swipe-up test on a re-created phone; one photo pressed into a paper bin; a tear-off day calendar clipped to the bin runs DAY 1 → DAY 30; Google's bin joins with its own calendar; the shared-album detour (a friend's copy stays); back to both bins: empty them; the bins and calendars are torn off whole, CAN'T GET IT BACK; frame 0 again. One mechanism (delete = move), the bin is in every beat after 9 s.
2. **A 30-day countdown as the whole film** (one giant calendar, a page per beat). Strong native move, but it would carry the friend's copy and the "really delete" step as text, not pictures, and frame 0 would be a calendar, not the viewer's photos.
3. **Cold open on the friend's phone** (the DM-able beat first). The share line is the best one to send, but it is a shared-album edge case; leading with it would make the film about shared albums, not the delete button everyone uses.

**Chosen: 1.** The viewer's own deleted photos on frame 0, the test they can do now, the calendar as the time jump a sound-off viewer follows, the friend's copy as the dip, the empty-it step as the takeaway.

## The native move
**The tear-off calendar.** A paper day calendar (a pad of cream pages on a dark board, ring-bound at the top) clips onto the Recently Deleted bin; on "thirty days" its pages tear off one by one and flutter down, DAY 1 → DAY 30. Every scene change is the same move: the whole scene is a calendar page that tears off along the perforation at the top (a jagged edge, left to right) and falls away, with the next scene already built under it. At the payoff the calendars are torn off whole.

## Rules for this film
- Coral only on the hero stamp CAN'T GET IT BACK and the YES / NO chips. The frame-0 delete marks, the ? tag and THEIRS STAYS are cream.
- No "60" anywhere, in voice or picture. Google's window is 30 days only (Google Photos Help, read Nov 9, 2026).
- The friend beat shows a shared album (an album card with two avatars, SHARED ALBUM), never a chat bubble, text, AirDrop or a screenshot.
- "Locked by default" carries its scope from its first frame: FACE ID OR TOUCH ID and BY DEFAULT · iOS 16+ · CAN BE TURNED OFF.
- "Can't get it back" is attributed in the voice and on screen (APPLE SUPPORT · GOOGLE PHOTOS HELP under the stamp); nothing about recovery tools or forensics.
- No quote strips: every on-screen phrase is a label, a credit or a re-created button, so nothing can be read as a misquote. The confirm buttons print Apple's and Google's own labels (Delete from All Devices, Empty Trash) with RE-CREATED on each sheet from its first frame.
- Generic phone drawn by hand (round punch-hole, no notch, no logo); the album screen and the confirm sheets are simplified re-creations, tagged RE-CREATED from their first frame. No Apple or Google logos, no real UI, no real photos: the snapshots are flat paper drawings (a dog, a beach, a sunset, a cake, a cat, a mountain).
- Every label, credit and stamp peels before the next subject lands; the hero hold (stamp, emptied bins, credits) is the only still ≥ 1.0 s.
- Lower band y 1000–1430 is filled in every second: the camera roll, the phone's lower half, the bins, the friend's phone, the confirm sheets.

## Changes in the build (2026-11-09)
- Frame 0's snapshot settles into its slot silently (0.12–0.37 s): Whisper pins the first word to 0.00 s, so a hit before it can't pass the SFX gate. The 320 px glide carries frame 0's motion.
- The hero stamp comes in raised on "can't" (its shadow apart) and slams down on the thump in the 1.0 s silence after "back." Both stamp lines and YES / NO need ≥ 1.8–2.0 s on screen and the end line is short, so the stamp can't wait for the silence to appear. The picture holds still 1.16 s after the slam (31.2–32.36 s); only the caption changes.
- The calendars are torn off whole on "can't" and "back", not on "Apple" / "Google", so the stage above the bins never sits empty before the stamp.
- The album's tear is on "delete the album"; the 1.1 s pause after it carries the page-tear rip into the confirm sheets.
- The end card arrives on "So," with YES / NO up at once; the headline returns after "no" and the snapshot lifts back over its slot, so the last frame is frame 0.

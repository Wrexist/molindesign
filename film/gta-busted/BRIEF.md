## 1. Topic and one hard idea
Getting busted in GTA 6 costs you four things you can protect ahead of time (looted guns, stolen cars, stash bags, pocket cash), and the biggest, up to $20,000 in your pocket, is the one most players still carry around.

- **Main search phrase:** **GTA 6 busted** (spoken in the hook by ~2 s: "G T A six … before you get busted"; frame 0 shows `BEFORE YOU GET BUSTED`). Secondary: "what happens when you get busted in GTA 6".
- **DM-able moment:** the `grand` line. A drawn $20,000 brick (the cap a character can carry) on the property counter, a cop's hand sweeps it off, stamp `KEPT BY POLICE`. The thing you send to the friend who's been driving around with $18k in his pocket.

## 2. Hook options
1. **"GTA 6: do these 4 before you get busted." + "Or they're gone for good."** Chosen. Isac's format verbatim (threat + count), the search phrase and "GTA 6" in the first second, and a promise the counter on screen makes literal (0/4). Frame 0: `BEFORE YOU GET BUSTED` (4 words) over Lucia in a jail jumpsuit (Trailer 1 plate). By 2.0 s the four empty slots on the property tag fill with icons (gun, car, bag, cash) and each gets a `CONFISCATED` mark: the payoff (what's at stake) on screen.
2. "The cops keep your twenty grand." Stronger number, but spends step 4 up front and doesn't promise a list (the format's retention engine is the climbing counter).
3. "Busted? Here's what you lose." Clear, but a definition-style opener (LESSONS: explainers flop) and no action.

**Words we chose and why**
- "The cops keep every dollar" (line `grand`): Game Rant (busted, F1) and GamesRadar (F5) both say all pocket cash goes on arrest; PC Gamer (F7) says "a few hundred". Two named guides vs one, and Game Rant's is the arrest-specific article, so the voice keeps it, and the `grand` card carries the source chip `GAME RANT · GAMESRADAR+`. If the director or jury wants zero risk, re-voice as "Guides say the cops keep all of it." (+0.4 s). Never say "Rockstar says".
- "Guns you loot", not "guns you pick up": GamesRadar's unowned class is enemy drops and world pickups; the gold-icon Unique Variants are an exception, so the `loot` card carries fine print `EXCEPT GOLD-ICON UNIQUE VARIANTS` (≥ 36 px).
- "Bought ones wait at Ammu-Nation or in your trunk": both GamesRadar and Game Rant name the Ammu-Nation weapons locker and the personal vehicle's trunk. "Most Ammu-Nation stores" in GamesRadar: the card says `WEAPON LOCKER · MOST AMMU-NATIONS`.
- "Register your ride at a Pay 'n' Spray": GamesRadar; costs a fee and cars with standard/advanced trackers can't be registered until later in the story. Card chip `FOR A FEE`. The voice doesn't promise it's free or always possible.
- "Stolen cars are gone after an arrest": Game Rant's busted article, word for word in meaning ("stolen or unregistered … vehicles will be gone").
- "Get stash bags to a fence first": Game Rant: stash bags "meant for fences" are confiscated when busted (and lost when wasted, F2). We don't say what's in them (no drug words, LESSONS/stashhouse).
- "But get busted in your own car? It's impounded.": GamesRadar (while driving it) and Game Rant (in or near it, "may"). The card says `IMPOUND LOT` and the plate shows our own drawn tow tag, no claim about how often.
- "Buying it back costs you." (added in jury r1 fix 10): GamesRadar+ personal-vehicles page, verbatim (re-read 2026-11-21): *"For a significant fee, you can request the ScooterBros services to recover your ride from the impound lot for you, but this may result in the loss of any stash bags you had hidden in your trunk."* No amount is given, so the screen shows a `$$$` tag and the card sub `PAY TO GET IT BACK`, never a number.
- "bank your cash": ATM or the BuckMe app after the Chapter 1 mission "Buck Me" (PowerPyx, VGC, GamesRadar). Not "your bank is 100 % safe": PowerPyx says some checking balance is lost on death and Game Rant names a small bailout fee from Chapter 2. Card fine print: `BANK = SAFER, NOT FREE · SMALL FEES FROM CH. 2`.
- "$20,000": per character (GamesRadar, PC Gamer). Spoken "twenty thousand dollars".

## 5. On-screen system and shots
**Our design, not CNS's** (his is a purple card with a gold outline and a gold STEP number; ours shares none of that): ink-dark stage, cream type, **one hot coral accent** (Delta pink-coral), plus a police-evidence look built by hand.
- **Pinned header chip** (top-left, all video from 0.3 s): `BEFORE YOU GET BUSTED` on a cream evidence-tape strip, ink type, a small coral siren dot that blinks once per step.
- **STEP n counter** (top-right, all video): a big stencilled numeral on a manila **property tag** with a string hole, `STEP 1` … `STEP 4`; the tag flips over (paper flip, 0.25 s, with a card-snap sound) on each "One/Two/Three/Four".
- **Native move: the property receipt.** A full-width jail **PROPERTY RECEIPT** sheet (cream paper, carbon-blue ruled lines, hand-ticked boxes) lives in the lower third (y ~1150–1430, bleeding the full width). It lists four rows from frame 1: `FIREARMS · VEHICLE · STASH BAGS · POCKET CASH`, each pre-stamped `CONFISCATED` in oxblood. Each step **tears its row off** the receipt with a perforation rip and the row's stamp flips to `KEPT` (coral). That's the payoff of each step, and the counter.
- **Progress counter** (on the receipt's footer): `KEPT 0/4` → `1/4` → … → `4/4` (slam to each value, never a roll; LESSONS gta-lawsuit). Goal gradient: the viewer wants the last row torn off.

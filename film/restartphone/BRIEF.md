## 1. Topic and one hard idea

**The NSA's phone guide says "Power the device off and on weekly", and a restart can clear spyware that can't survive one, but the NSA's own chart says it only sometimes prevents the attack, the spyware can come back, and only an update closes the hole; so update, and restart weekly.**

**Backlog hook kept, cut to 5 words (LESSONS wifibattery: the hook follows the source's strength).** "The NSA says restart your phone weekly." is 7 words (limit 6). It is a plain attribution of the NSA's own words, so it doesn't overclaim; it only has to be shortened: **"The NSA says restart weekly."** (5). The film's turn is that the source itself scopes its advice ("Sometimes prevents"), so the hook is true and the twist is still the source's.

**The backlog's "why" made concrete.** "It can clear some malware that lives only in memory" is not in the NSA sheet. The sourced version: Kaspersky's 2023 report on an iPhone spyware campaign says "The malicious toolset does not support persistence" and the phones "may be reinfected after rebooting". So the voice says "Some spyware can't survive a restart" (Kaspersky) and "a reboot **can** wipe it out" (can, never "stops hackers"). "Lives only in memory" stays out of the voice; it is an inference.

**The backlog's "updates matter more" made concrete.** No source I read ranks them. So the voice doesn't rank them: it says a restart "doesn't close the hole" and "An update can", and shows the dated proof: Apple's iOS 16.5.1 (June 21, 2023) fixed a kernel flaw credited to Kaspersky's researchers, "actively exploited against versions of iOS released before iOS 15.7".

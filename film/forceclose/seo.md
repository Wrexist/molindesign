Closing your apps won't save battery. Swiping apps out of the app switcher feels like cleanup, but most of those apps are already paused, and an app you swipe away has to start from scratch next time.

What Apple says. Apple Support, "Close an app on your iPhone or iPod touch" (published Oct 15, 2026): "You should close an app only if it's unresponsive." And: "If your iPhone seems to run slowly or its battery-life drains too quickly, closing all of your apps does not impact your device's overall performance. Recently used apps only use resources and power when they're active or when they're performing specific background tasks or services."

Most are paused. Android Developers, "Processes and app lifecycle": "A well-running system has multiple cached processes always available, for efficient switching between applications, and regularly kills the cached apps as needed." And: "Since cached processes can be killed by the system at any time, apps should cease all work while in the cached state."

From scratch. Android Developers, "App startup time": "In a cold start, your app starts from scratch." That's the hardest kind of launch "because the system and app have more work to do than in the other launch states." More work, not less.

No and no. In March 2016 a 9to5Mac reader emailed Tim Cook asking whether he quits his apps and whether that's necessary for battery life. Craig Federighi, Apple's senior VP of Software Engineering, wrote back "no and no" (as reported by 9to5Mac and MacRumors, Mar 10, 2016).

What drains it. Apple, "Maximizing Battery Life and Lifespan": "There are two simple ways you can preserve battery life — no matter how you use your device: adjust your screen brightness and use Wi-Fi." Apple's battery messages include "No Cell Coverage and Low Signal" and "Background Activity. This indicates that the battery was used by the app while it was in the background — that is, while you were using another app." For that one, Apple's fix is Settings › General › Background App Refresh, not swiping the app away.

Check yours. iPhone: "Open Settings. Tap Battery. Tap View All Battery Usage to view the battery usage percentage over the last eight days." (Apple Support, "Check battery usage on your iPhone", Mar 25, 2026). Pixel: Settings › Battery › Battery usage, then View by apps (Google Pixel Help, "Fix battery drain problem").

When to close one. Apple: "If an app won't respond or seems frozen, you can close it, then open it again." Google: "If your phone's battery life seems poor or short, it could be that a downloaded app uses too much battery." Pixel Help says to restrict that app first, then force stop it if needed, and warns: "If you force stop some essential apps or services, your phone won't work correctly."

So, still swiping them all away? Yes or no.

Fine print: "most" apps are paused, not all; Apple says recently used apps use power "when they're performing specific background tasks or services", so music, navigation and downloads can keep running. "More work" comes from Android's page on startup time, which gives no battery figure; no first-party source we found puts a number on what reopening an app costs, so this video gives none.

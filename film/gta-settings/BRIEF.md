**Truth-proofing (read first).**
- **Rockstar's own settings page could not be read** (support.rockstargames.com returned 503 / empty reply on every try, 2026-11-20). What we know of it comes from search snippets and Player.One's summary: Settings is opened from the Pause Menu, and "Display covers visual effects and high-contrast shaders". So the setting names, defaults and ranges below rest on PC Gamer (reputable, hands-on) plus guides and launch-day menu screenshots. Every default printed on screen must be one that **two** sources agree on (table §3).
- **Motion blur default is 3, max 10** (PC Gamer, hands-on, 2026-11-19). The TikTok's "5 before" was his own setting, not the default: never print 5 as default. **Minimum 0** rests on the TikTok's own screenshot plus BGR and Gamerbolt ("lower the slider to zero"); gamer.org's "0–3" and playday's "3 to 10" ranges are wrong against PC Gamer and are not used.
- **"Zero can look choppy"** is hedged on purpose: Rockstar targets 30 fps on consoles; Gamerbolt and TheTechBasket say blur hides some of the gap between frames at 30 fps; PC Gamer's Jacob Ridley tried the "more blur hides judder" idea, rejected it, and "landed on setting motion blur to 1 on the standard PS5, as it feels the cleanest overall". The voice says "a PC Gamer editor picked one", which is exactly his line. Never say "PC Gamer recommends" (it is one editor's pick, his own words "feels the cleanest").
- **Effects sliders:** we say only the names (the three sliders), their place (Display, Visual Effects) and that they sit at 5 by default (playday "1-5, 5 default" for Player Status and Hit; TheTechBasket's launch-day screenshot shows all three at 5; the TikTok's "before" also shows 5). **Awareness default rests on the screenshot only**, so the on-screen card prints "5 → 2" as the viral tip's numbers, not a DEFAULT tag. **We do not say what each slider does**: no source describes the effect. "Your call" = taste, our verdict KEEP.
- **HDR:** the in-game HDR Calibration exists with Max Brightness, Black Levels and Paper White (Gamerbolt; the TikTok's screenshot; playday lists "HDR Calibration"). Gamerbolt: "Maximum Brightness: This should correspond to your television's actual HDR capabilities." That is also how HDR peak brightness works on any game. TheTechBasket says no launch-day source listed an in-game HDR screen; outweighed by the screenshot plus Gamerbolt, but the card avoids drawing Rockstar's calibration image (no logo).
- **Brightness Reduction:** On/Off, **Off by default** (playday, gamer.org; the TikTok's "before" shows Off). What it does is **not documented anywhere we found**. gamer.org: it "may help players who find intense lighting effects uncomfortable"; Beebom's own two tables disagree (Off in one, On in the other). So the voice gives **our pick**, labeled OUR PICK on screen: "Leave it off unless bright light bothers you." Never say it "darkens", "ruins" or "doesn't work".
- **"One survives"** is our scorecard, not a fact: KEEP (effects), FIX (blur → try 1), FIX (HDR → your TV), SKIP (Brightness Reduction). The scorecard card carries `OUR VERDICTS`.

## 1. One hard idea
A viral "make GTA 6 look insane" settings list has four tips, and only one holds up as is: GTA 6 runs at 30 fps, so zero blur can look choppy, HDR numbers belong to the poster's TV, and Brightness Reduction ships off for a reason.

Main search phrase: **GTA 6 settings** (spoken in the hook at ~1 s; frame 0 shows `GTA 6 · SETTINGS`). Secondary: GTA 6 motion blur.

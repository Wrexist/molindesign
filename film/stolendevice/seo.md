Can a thief who saw your iPhone passcode lock you out? Apple's support article "About Stolen Device Protection for iPhone" (Published Date: October 22, 2026) says the feature's extra steps "help prevent someone who has stolen your iPhone and knows your passcode from making critical changes to your account or device."

What it adds, in Apple's iOS 17.3 notes: "Stolen Device Protection increases security of iPhone and Apple ID by requiring Face ID or Touch ID with no passcode fallback to perform certain actions." With the setting off, your passcode works for those actions like it always has.

With it on, when your iPhone is "away from familiar locations such as home or work":
- Face ID or Touch ID, "with no passcode alternative or fallback," for actions on Apple's list such as "Use passwords or passkeys saved in Keychain", "Turn off Lost Mode" and "Erase all content and settings".
- A Security Delay for changes such as "Change your Apple Account password", "Change your passcode" and "Turn off Stolen Device Protection". Apple: these "require you to wait an hour and then perform an additional Face ID or Touch ID authentication."

Why the hour? Apple: "the security delay is designed to prevent a thief from performing critical operations so that you can mark your device as lost and make sure your Apple Account is secure."

The fine print, also from Apple:
- "When your iPhone is in a familiar location, these additional steps are not required, and you can use your iPhone passcode like usual." You can also set it to always require them.
- "Your iPhone may end the security delay early after it detects that you've arrived at a familiar location."
- "You can still use your iPhone passcode for purchases with Apple Pay."
- "Stolen Device Protection is available with iOS 17.3 or later and must be turned on before the device is lost or stolen."
- "Stolen Device Protection might be turned on by default."

Check yours: Settings › Face ID & Passcode (Touch ID & Passcode on iPhones with Touch ID) › enter your passcode › Stolen Device Protection. To turn it on, Apple says you need two-factor authentication for your Apple Account, a device passcode, Face ID or Touch ID, Significant Locations, and Find My turned on.

  **Reply:** No, after the hour Apple asks for a second Face ID or Touch ID scan, and the thief doesn't have your face or finger. The hour is for you: Apple says the delay is there "so that you can mark your device as lost and make sure your Apple Account is secure."

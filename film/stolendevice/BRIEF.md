## 1. Topic and one hard idea

**Apple's own support page says Stolen Device Protection exists to "help prevent someone who has stolen your iPhone and knows your passcode from making critical changes": away from familiar places like home or work, saved passwords need Face ID or Touch ID with no passcode fallback, and changing your Apple Account password needs a scan, an hour's wait and a second scan, so you have time to mark the phone lost; it has to be on before the theft, so check yours.**

**Backlog hook rewritten.** "A thief with your passcode wins." overclaims twice: Apple says nothing about how often thieves win, and with the feature on (it "might be turned on by default") the film's whole point is that they don't. Chosen: **"They watched you type your passcode."** (6): a story opener, a stake, no claim to overclaim. The question (`ask`) and the low point (`keys`, `dip`) are both scoped "with one setting off".

**Scope in the voice, not only in small print (LESSONS restartphone, rice, #41 findableoff, #42 publicwifi):**
- "With one setting off" (`keys`): the threat lines are only about an iPhone without the feature.
- "Away from home or work" (`away`): Apple's own phrase for familiar locations.
- "your face or fingerprint" (`away`), "a scan" (`delay`): Face ID **or** Touch ID, never "Face ID" alone in the voice except the Settings name in `check`.
- "Apple Pay still takes your passcode, though." (`pay`): the backlog's required caveat, in the voice.
- "It must be on before the theft." (`end`): Apple's "must be turned on before the device is lost or stolen".
- No crime figures, no "thieves do this all the time", no named victim, no WSJ numbers (paywalled; secondary only).

1. **"They watched you type your passcode."** (chosen, 6) A story in one line, a person implied but never drawn (we show the passcode dots being seen, not the watcher). It sets the stake without asserting anything Apple doesn't say, and the film answers it.

## 5. Open questions and truth risks for the director

1. **"lock you out" (`ask`).** It's a question, answered by the film; Apple's page never uses "lock you out". If the jury reads it as a claim, keep the voice and add the scope plate earlier (on `ask`, not `keys`).
2. **`keys` "it opens your saved passwords" with the setting off.** Supported by Apple's own "no passcode alternative or fallback" being the feature's addition (A3, B14) and A12 ("use your iPhone passcode like usual"). The passcode fallback still exists at home with the feature on; the film says so through the HOME / WORK circle, not the voice.
3. **`check` says "Face ID and Passcode"** in the voice; on Touch ID iPhones the row is "Touch ID & Passcode". The chip **OR TOUCH ID & PASSCODE** carries that. If the jury docks it, `say` could become "Settings, Face ID or Touch ID, and Passcode" (+3 words, 98 total, over the 95 cap), so prefer the chip.
4. **Search phrase timing.** The spoken phrase (`ask`) starts at ~4.6–5.0 s at Ming pace; the on-screen search bar from 2.8 s covers the first-5-s rule. If the voice comes back slow, drop `hook` pause to 0.3.
5. **Ming risks.** `took` is a short line (5 words; Kokoro/Ming lesson on short lines): if it reads weak, take 1, else merge pause 0.4 → 0.3. `twist` ends on "on" after a name: 0.35 tail fade so "Protection on" doesn't blur into "Protection". `delay` has "a scan... an hour... another scan." in `say` for the three beats; drop the ellipses if Ming over-holds. `pay` starts with "Apple Pay" (a name at a line start, LESSONS gps): check Whisper hears it; fallback "And Apple Pay still takes your passcode." (+1 word, 96, then cut `end` to "It must be on first. Is yours?"). "Passcode" in `check` is Apple's capitalized row name, fine for captions.
6. **Title.** Backlog title "Stolen Device Protection Stops Passcode Thieves 😱" says "Stops", which is stronger than Apple's "help prevent". SEO should use **"Stolen Device Protection Slows Passcode Thieves 😱"** or **"Your iPhone Passcode Isn't Enough Anymore 😱"**; flag "Stops" as a truth risk.
7. **Planned silences:** 0.4 s after `hook` (slap), 0.4 after `took` (slide stop), 0.9 after `dip` (thud), 0.4 after `twist` (switch click), 1.1 after `delay` (hero stamp, screenshot hold), 0.4 after `check` (switch on). Hits at line end + 0.3 s, margin 0.2; layer 1–3 kHz, ≥ 3 dB under the voice.

## 1. Topic and one hard idea

**On its own, a chatbot doesn't calculate: it predicts the answer the way it predicts words. OpenAI's tokenizer reads a long number in chunks of up to three digits, from the left, which aren't the groups your commas make, and the model writes the answer from the left, piece by piece, while carrying runs from the right. So the first piece is a guess. Newer models slip far less, and many can now hand big sums to a calculator, in code.**

Story order: the claim (your chatbot doesn't calculate) → the condition and the question (on its own; why is AI bad at math?) → the mechanism (chunks, like words; this number becomes three pieces, measured) → the mismatch (not where your commas go) → the direction problem (it writes from the left; you carry from the right) → **hero: the first piece is a guess (GUESSED)** → honest update (newer models slip far less) → the fix (a calculator, in code) → the viewer's own chatbot → yes or no, looping into line 1.

**Native move for this film: the cut.** Numbers are paper tape; the tokenizer is a pair of scissors; every token is a tile. Part 2 (Strawberry) said "it sees tokens"; this film shows where the scissors fall on a number, which a viewer can check with OpenAI's own tool.

**Main search phrase: `why is AI bad at math`** (spoken inside `ask`, ≈ 3.5–5.5 s). Show **WHY IS AI BAD AT MATH?** in the upper third from ≈ 1.9 s and in the caption's first line. Secondary phrases for the SEO agent (volumes not checked): `ChatGPT math`, `can ChatGPT do math`, `AI tokens numbers`, `why does ChatGPT get math wrong`, `tokenizer`.

**DM-able moment:** "ChatGPT's tokenizer cuts 1999999 into 199 / 999 / 9" (the scissors frame; anyone can paste it into OpenAI's tokenizer and see). Second: "just adding commas made GPT-3.5's sums up to 20 % more accurate" (pinned comment, §5).

**Description** (4017 characters, under the 4,500 cap; no < or > anywhere; opens with the answer; every quote word for word; fine print covers dated plan terms, no ranking, no speed number, separate caps out of scope, hand-made re-creations, not affiliated). Paste it in full:

```
Why does unlimited data slow down? "Unlimited" is about how much data you can use, not how fast it goes. Every cell tower has a fixed amount of capacity. When a tower is busy, your carrier decides whose data goes first, and your plan sets your place in that line. On some plans you move back after a set number of gigs, until your next bill starts. Most of the time you won't notice. And it's written down: your plan's Broadband Facts label links the page that explains it.

The tower. T-Mobile, on every wireless provider: "there is a fixed amount of network capacity available at each cell tower." And: "In the vast majority of times and places, you will notice little if any difference."

When it's busy. AT&T, data usage support: "On an unlimited plan? We may temporarily slow your speed at any time if our network is busy." Verizon, network performance: "On certain plans, we may prioritize your data behind other traffic." And: "Once the demand on the site lessens, or if you connect to a different site not experiencing high demand, your speed will return to normal."

After 50 gigs, on some plans. AT&T: "We may also slow it after you use more than 50GB or 22GB of data in a single bill period." And: "things will go back to normal when your next bill period starts." T-Mobile, plan terms: "T-Mix Saver and T-Mix Standard customers may notice reduced speeds during congestion until next bill cycle after monthly premium data allotment, due to data prioritization." Other plans use other numbers, or none. Check yours.

How to check. The FCC requires a broadband label. 47 CFR 8.1: providers "shall create and display an accurate broadband consumer label for each stand-alone broadband internet access service it currently offers for purchase," and those with online account portals "shall also make each customer's label easily accessible to the customer in such portals." FCC 22-86 (Nov 2022): "We require that ISPs include in the label a link to their network management practices." FCC 26-48 (Jul 2026): "We retain the requirement that providers that give their customers access to their account information via an online portal must give those customers easy access to the label of their current plan." So: open your carrier account, find the Broadband Facts label for your plan, tap Network Management, and look for the number of premium or high-speed gigs.

So, did you think unlimited meant full speed? Yes or no.

Fine print: Plan terms change often. Everything here was checked on Nov 12, 2026, and applies only to the plans named. This film does not rank carriers or say any plan is better or worse, and it gives no speed for when you are moved back, because the carriers don't publish one number. Some plans also have separate limits (hard caps, video quality, hotspot) that are not covered here; your label and plan terms list them. The label, quote strips, phone and pages shown are re-created by hand in the FCC label format; no real interface or logo is shown. Not affiliated with AT&T, T-Mobile, Verizon or the FCC.
```

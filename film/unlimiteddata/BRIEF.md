## 1. Topic and one hard idea

**"Unlimited" is about how much data you can use, not how fast it goes: every cell tower has a fixed amount of capacity, so when one is busy your carrier decides whose data goes first, your plan sets your place in that line, on some plans you move back after a set number of gigs until the next bill, and your plan's Broadband Facts label links the page that spells it out.**

**Backlog hook replaced.** "Your unlimited data has a limit." is defensible (Verizon's Simplicity plan cuts speeds to 4 Mbps after 500 GB; every plan has a hotspot limit), but the film is about **speed when the tower is busy**, not an amount cap. "Has a limit" invites "no it doesn't, T-Mobile says unlimited is truly unlimited" (T-Mobile's own words, fact 4) and sets up a cap the film never pays off. The new hook names the actual mechanism and is true of every big-three unlimited plan by their own pages: AT&T "On an unlimited plan? We may temporarily slow your speed at any time if our network is busy." (fact 1).

## 5. Open questions and truth risks for the director

1. **Length.** Formula 36.2 s, `dur` 36.5; expect 33–36 s. Over 38 s: cut "in a month" from `dip` (the AFTER 50 GB card carries it), then shorten `but` to "But it's written down." (drop "and you can check").
2. **The `dip` chips (truth risk: "don't name a carrier as worse").** Chips with each carrier's own number could read as a comparison, so the two chips are both 50 GB lines (AT&T's own "50GB or 22GB", T-Mobile's T-Mix Standard 50GB) and AT&T's current Extra 2.0 (100GB) and Value 2.0 (5GB) stay off screen. Rules: equal size, alphabetical, all cream, each the carrier's own words, no Verizon chip with a number unless it says the same kind of thing (Verizon Unlimited Welcome is slowed "during times of congestion" with no gig threshold, so its chip would read "ALWAYS AT THE BACK", a worse-looking claim we can't scope cleanly; leave Verizon out of the dip chips, and give Verizon the hero sentence instead so no carrier is singled out). **Simpler option if it reads busy:** one chip only, **e.g. T-MIX STANDARD: 50GB PREMIUM DATA · T-MOBILE · NOV 2026**, next to **SOME PLANS**, plus AT&T's own older-plan "50GB" line, since both carriers use 50 GB.
3. **Hero policy sentence: whose?** Verizon's ("On certain plans, we may prioritize your data behind other traffic.") is the clearest one-liner and balances the AT&T/T-Mobile chips earlier. Keep "On certain plans" in the quote; never cut it (LESSONS stepbystep: a dropped hedge costs a Truth point).
4. **"Every cell tower has a fixed amount of room."** T-Mobile says it of every provider's towers. Capacity varies by tower and spectrum; "fixed" means a tower has a ceiling, not that all towers are equal. Don't draw two towers with the same pipe as a comparison.
5. **"your carrier decides whose data goes first"** must never read as "always slow". Keep **ONLY WHEN THE TOWER IS BUSY** visible through `busy` and `place`. T-Mobile: "little, if any, effect … in the vast majority of times and locations".
6. **Label truth.** Draw the label in the FCC format, not any carrier's label; no prices, no speeds, no plan ID. The FCC requires a label "for each stand-alone broadband internet access service it currently offers for purchase", and the portal link to "the label for their current plan" (FCC 26-48 ¶22). Don't claim "every plan ever" has one. FCC 26-48 removes the template from the CFR and its amendments wait for OMB, so don't print a rule number on the label itself; credit **47 CFR 8.1 · FCC 26-48 (JUL 2026)** in the corner.
7. **No "throttling", no "deprioritize" on screen or in the voice.** Carrier words only: "may temporarily slow", "prioritize … behind other traffic", "reduced speeds during congestion".
8. **No ✓/OK chips.** Nothing says "SAFE", "FULL SPEED ✓" or ticks a plan. The takeaway chip says what to look for, not which plan is fine.
9. **Planned silences.** 0.5 s after `hook` (frame-0 ≠ hit; LESSONS: hook hit needs ≥ 0.45 s), 0.6 s after `bill` (NEXT BILL CYCLE flap), 1.2 s after `hero` (marker circle + screenshot hold). `place` has 0.45 s for the ticket print tick. Keep them through re-timing.
10. **Ming risks.** "Broadband Facts" (listed in `names`; check unhinted Whisper hears it, not "broadband fax"). "gigs" after "fifty" (check it isn't heard "gig"). `ask` and `yn` carry commas in `say` for pace, as in airplanemode; `ask` has no comma between verb and object.

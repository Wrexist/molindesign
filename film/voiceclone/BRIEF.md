## 1. Topic and one hard idea

**To a voice-cloning model, your voice is just numbers: Microsoft's VALL-E turned a 3-second recording into codes, predicted new codes for new words, and played them back in that voice. A copy of the numbers carries your sound but not your secrets, which is why the FBI tells families to agree on a secret word.**

Story order: the stake (only three seconds) → the question with the search phrase (how does AI voice cloning work?) → the checkable proof (Microsoft's own 2023 paper) → the mechanism in two steps (3 s → 1,800 numbers; predict numbers for new words) → it can sound like you (with the paper's condition) → **hero: to the model, your voice is just numbers** (stamp + hold) → the dated stake (FBI PSA, Dec 3, 2024) → the tip (secret word, same page) → why it works (the copy has your sound, not your secret) → the question that loops into line 1.

**Native move for this film: the voice tape.** A paper roll of number tiles that feeds out of a mic, keeps going past a pencil line, and plays through a speaker. It is hand-made, readable with the sound off, and it *is* the mechanism, since VALL-E's pipeline is literally sound → codes → more codes → sound.

**Main search phrase: `AI voice cloning`** (spoken in `ask`, ≈ 2.6–5.1 s on the chain, about 2.3–4.4 s with Ming). Show **HOW DOES AI VOICE CLONING WORK?** in the upper third from ≈ 2.0 s, and put it in the caption's first line. Secondary phrases for the SEO agent (volumes not checked): `ai voice clone scam`, `family safe word`, `secret word scam call`, `vall-e 3 seconds`, `fbi ai voice warning`, `grandparent scam ai voice`.

**DM-able moment:** "The FBI says to make a secret word with your family, because AI can copy a voice from a short clip." This is the one people send to their parents. Second: "Microsoft's research model needed three seconds."

## 2. Hook options

1. **"Only three seconds of your voice."** (chosen; 6 words; frame 0 `ONLY 3 SECONDS`, 3 words) This is the paper's own phrase ("only a 3-second enrolled recording"), so the hook and the proof are one source, as in incognito. As a fragment it opens a puzzle ("…to do what?") without claiming more than the source: the paper says what VALL-E *did* in research, and the voice never says "enough to fool anyone". It sets up exactly the mechanism the body explains: a short clip becomes numbers.
2. "Three seconds of your voice is enough." (the backlog hook, rejected as written) "Is enough" claims a result for every voice and every use. The source itself says similarity depends on the prompt's length and quality, and VALL-E is a research demo, not a released tool. It would fail Truth.
3. "AI copied voices from three seconds." (6 words, rejected) Checkable, but "copied" with no scope reads as a product anyone can use today, and it hands the answer to the hook. It is also third person, while the stats favour "your".

**Wording choices tied to sources**
- `hook` "Only three seconds of your voice.": fact 1, "only a 3-second enrolled recording". "Your" is the second-person frame (an "unseen speaker" is anyone not in training). The scope chips carry RESEARCH DEMO.
- `ask` is the search phrase as a question.
- `paper` "It's in Microsoft's own 2023 paper.": fact 1 (Microsoft authors, Jan 5, 2023). "It's" is the lead-in word before the name (LESSONS gps).
- `numbers` "First, those three seconds become 1,800 numbers.": fact 3, our math from the paper's 750 × 8 per 10 s, labelled OUR MATH on screen. "Numbers" = the paper's "discrete codes" (each an index 0–1023).
- `predict` "Then it predicts numbers for words you never said.": fact 3 (the language model estimates the code matrix from the phoneme sequence of the new text plus the 3-second prompt; "next token"). "Words you never said" = the target text, not in the prompt.
- `sound` "Played back, they can sound like you.": fact 3 (the codec decoder synthesizes speech) and fact 2 ("speak in a voice like the voice talent"), with "can" for fact 2's condition. The condition is on screen.
- `hero` "To the model, your voice is just numbers.": the film's summary of facts 1 and 3 (codes in, codes out). Our words.
- `fbi` "In 2024, the FBI warned criminals fake loved ones' voices.": fact 5, "warned", paraphrasing "Criminals generate short audio clips containing a loved one's voice to impersonate a close relative". The exact quote is on the folder.
- `tip` "Its tip: a secret word with your family.": fact 5, "Create a secret word or phrase with your family to verify their identity."
- `secret` "The copy has your sound, not your secret.": our plain-words reason the tip works (the clip gives the model your sound; a word agreed in private isn't in it). No quote marks. It claims nothing about detection.
- `end` asks the viewer; it makes no claim.

**Kept out on purpose**
- Any tool, app or service name, and any step a viewer could follow to clone a voice (backlog note). Commercial cloning products and their clip lengths were not researched and are not named.
- "Anyone can do this today", "it fools everyone", "perfect copy": VALL-E is a research demo, and the page conditions similarity on the clip.
- VALL-E 2's "human parity" claim: a second model and a second idea (it is a benchmark claim); pinned-comment material at most.
- Loss figures, victim counts and named cases: none was read from a primary source today. The FTC said in 2023 that it had no data on how many scams used voice cloning (search summary; not re-read on the page), so no numbers are given.

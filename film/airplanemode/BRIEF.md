## 1. Topic and one hard idea

**Airplane mode answers two rules from two agencies: the FAA's (keep the plane's radios and navigation clean, and in 2013 its panel found most airliners can tolerate phones, except some landing systems in low visibility) and the FCC's 1991 ban on cell signals in the air, which protects cell towers on the ground, and which the FCC kept for your phone last month while opening the same band to drones.**

**Backlog hook replaced.** "Your phone won't crash the plane. So why airplane mode?" claims something no primary source says. The FAA's own panel says "most" airplanes tolerate PEDs and that some landing systems "may not be proven to tolerate the interference"; UK CAA lab tests (2003) saw compass, navigation and headset-audio anomalies. "Won't crash" would be our claim, not theirs, and it's the kind of absolute a juror docks. The new hook ("Airplane mode isn't only for planes.") is true by the FAA's own words (AC 91.21-1D §9.1 ties the cellular rule to "ground-based cellular stations").

## 2. Hook options

2. "Your phone won't crash the plane." (backlog, 6 words, rejected) No primary source says "won't"; FAA says "most" airplanes tolerate PEDs and names a low-visibility exception.
3. "Drones can use it. Your phone can't." (7 words, rejected as the opener, kept as the DM beat) Current and surprising, but it needs the FCC band explained first and is 7 words; as frame 0 it would be a riddle without a stake.

**Kept out on purpose (truth)**
- **"Your phone can't/won't crash a plane" / "it's perfectly safe."** No primary source says so. FAA: "most" airplanes tolerate PEDs, with a low-visibility exception.
- **"Phones have caused crashes."** No source here; CAA's 35 incidents are reports "linked" by crews, and CAA itself notes many "question whether a genuine problem exists".
- **The pilot-headset buzz.** Sourced only to UK CAA 2003 (reports of "noise in the flight crew headphones" and lab "background noise on audio outputs", older GSM-era phones and general-aviation equipment). - **5G and radar altimeters (the 2022 C-band story).** That was about ground 5G towers near airports, not phones on board. Don't mix it in.
- **In-flight calls.** DOT and Congress proposals exist (unconfirmed 2026 "Quiet Skies Act" in secondary sources only). Not used.
- **Fines/enforcement amounts.** The rule's posted notice says "suspension of service and/or a fine"; no amount or case found. Not used.
- **Europe allows it.** EASA (2014) lets airlines allow phones on after an assessment, and the EU has 5G on some planes, but it's a second region and dilutes a US film (LESSONS gta-gators: one place, one stake).
